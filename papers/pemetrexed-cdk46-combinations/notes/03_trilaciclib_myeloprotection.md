# Trilaciclib + chemotherapy — myeloprotection

Trilaciclib (Cosela, G1T28) is an intravenous, short-acting CDK4/6 inhibitor used in a fundamentally different way from oral CDK4/6i: it is dosed **transiently, before each chemotherapy infusion**, to push hematopoietic stem/progenitor cells (HSPCs) — which are RB-proficient and CDK4/6-dependent — into temporary G1 arrest so cytotoxic chemotherapy spares them (**myelopreservation**). This is the largest bucket in the collection (63 papers) and is directly relevant to "pemetrexed + CDK4/6i combinations," because trilaciclib is administered alongside platinum/gemcitabine/etoposide/topotecan regimens and the same G1-arrest mechanism that protects marrow could, in principle, blunt an S-phase antimetabolite in the tumor if the tumor is also RB-proficient.

## Mechanistic foundation
- **Bisi et al. 2016 (PMID 26826116):** preclinical characterization of G1T28 — selective, reversible CDK4/6i providing multilineage marrow protection **without reducing chemotherapy efficacy against RB1-deficient tumors** (the key selectivity concept: protect RB-intact marrow, don't shield RB-null tumor).
- **He et al. 2017 (PMID 28446688):** transient CDK4/6 inhibition protects murine HSCs from chemotherapy-induced **exhaustion** in a serial 5-FU model — faster count recovery, preserved transplantation capacity.
- **Lai et al. 2020 (PMID 33004541):** beyond marrow protection, trilaciclib **enhances T-cell activation and antitumor immunity**, augmenting chemo/immune-checkpoint combinations preclinically and in SCLC patients.

## Pivotal clinical trials
- **Weiss et al. 2019 — Phase Ib/II, ES-SCLC + etoposide/carboplatin (PMID 31504118):** improved neutrophil/RBC/lymphocyte measures; fewer ≥G3 AEs (50% vs 83.8%), driven by less hematologic toxicity; antitumor efficacy preserved.
- **Daniel et al. 2021 — Phase II, ES-SCLC + carboplatin/etoposide/atezolizumab (PMID 33348420):** highly significant reductions in duration and occurrence of severe neutropenia (occurrence 1.9% vs 49.1%); improved RBC/platelet measures and HRQoL; comparable antitumor outcomes.
- **Hart et al. 2021 — Phase II, ES-SCLC + topotecan (PMID 33123968):** reduced severe neutropenia across a second-line myelosuppressive regimen.
- **Tan et al. 2019 / 2022 — Phase II, metastatic TNBC + gemcitabine/carboplatin (PMID 31575503, 34887261):** trilaciclib before GCb not only protected marrow but **significantly prolonged OS** (HR ~0.37 combined), with greater benefit in PD-L1-positive disease and enhanced T-cell activation — the strongest efficacy signal for the class.
- **Lenz et al. 2025 — PRESERVE 1, Phase III, mCRC + FOLFOXIRI/bevacizumab (PMID 39579142)** and **Goel et al. 2022 — PRESERVE 2, Phase III, mTNBC + GCb (PMID 36135712):** confirmatory Phase III programs extending the strategy to additional regimens.

## Approval and real-world evidence
- **Trilaciclib: First Approval, 2021 (PMID 33861388)** and FDA-approval commentary (PMID 34419683) — approved to reduce chemotherapy-induced myelosuppression in ES-SCLC receiving platinum/etoposide- or topotecan-containing regimens.
- Numerous real-world and meta-analytic confirmations in ES-SCLC (e.g., PMID 37490258, 41133004, 40337879), esophageal chemoradiotherapy (PMID 40232146, 40277746), and pooled solid-tumor analyses (PMID 42028439, 41642397).
- Pharmacology: dose-selection and PK/PD (PMID 33595690, 36180417), drug–drug interaction (PMID 35842567), hepatic impairment (PMID 38639103).
- Emerging biology: trilaciclib sensitizes NSCLC to anti-PD-L1 via a neutrophil-related immune response (PMID 41197618); CDK4/6 inhibition mitigates chemotherapy-induced expansion of TP53-mutant clonal hematopoiesis (PMID 41814002).

## Relevance to a pemetrexed + CDK4/6i strategy
1. **Trilaciclib is the clinically validated way to give a CDK4/6i with cytotoxic chemotherapy** — and pemetrexed/platinum regimens are natural candidates for myeloprotection (pemetrexed is myelosuppressive).
2. **The selectivity caveat is central:** trilaciclib is designed to protect RB-proficient marrow while *not* shielding RB-deficient tumors. In an **RB-proficient tumor** (which is where oral CDK4/6i anti-tumor synergy with pemetrexed would be sought), a continuous CDK4/6i could theoretically protect the tumor from an S-phase antimetabolite — the antagonism risk discussed in `04_mechanism_antimetabolite_interactions.md`. Transient pre-chemo dosing (trilaciclib) vs. continuous dosing (oral CDK4/6i) is therefore a key design fork.
3. **Immune enhancement** from transient CDK4/6 inhibition is a bonus that could complement chemo/IO backbones used with pemetrexed in nonsquamous NSCLC.
