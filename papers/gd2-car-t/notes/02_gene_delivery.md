# Gene delivery and integration platforms

## 1. Gamma-retroviral platforms

### 1.1 SFG (MoMLV-derived, spliced)

- The Brenner / Baylor lineage of GD2 CAR-T (Pule 2008 PMID 18978797; Louis 2011 PMID 21984804; Heczey 2014 PMID 25049283; Heczey 2017 PMID 28602436; Tian 2025 PMID 40044579) uses the SFG retroviral backbone, originally derived from a Moloney murine leukemia virus splicing vector kindly provided by R.C. Mulligan. Pule 2008 (Methods, p. 449–490) describes the construction in detail: the GD2 scFv-Zeta cassette is cloned between NcoI and BamHI sites in SFG.lacZ. Two PCR-distinguishable variants (Zeta-5 / Zeta-6) were generated by inserting a 12-bp tag with either an MluI or SphI restriction site between the stop codon and the 3' LTR, allowing the team to distinguish in-vivo persistence of CAR-CTLs vs CAR-ATCs within the same patient by PCR amplicon-cutting.
- **Producer cell line**: PG13 (gibbon-ape leukemia virus packaging line, ATCC CRL-10686). Phoenix-Eco is transfected with the SFG.GD2 construct to generate a transient pseudotyped supernatant, which is then used to serially transduce PG13, with the highest-titer clone selected for clinical GMP banking (Pule 2008 Methods; Heczey 2017 "Clinical Grade Vector Production").

### 1.2 MSGV-1 (Murine Stem-cell Virus / MSCV LTR)

- Used by the NCI (Rosenberg / Maloney / Mackall) and Stanford lineages. Long 2015 (Methods, lines 670–700) explicitly describes constructing `MSGV-14g2a-28z` by:
  - Signal peptide (residues 1–19 of CD8α) →
  - 14g2a scFv (provided by M.K. Brenner) →
  - Human IgG1 CH2CH3 spacer →
  - CD28 TM + CD28 cytoplasmic →
  - CD3ζ cytoplasmic.
  Then re-derives the BBz form by replacing the CD28 endodomain with 4-1BB.
- Mount 2018 (PMID 29662203 Methods, lines 506–532): the GD2-4-1BB-CD3ζ CAR is constructed by replacing the CD19 scFv in a previously-described CD19-4-1BBz vector with the 14g2a scFv, then packaged by transient transfection of 293GP with the CAR plasmid + RD114 envelope using Lipofectamine 2000 on poly-D-lysine plates. Supernatants are harvested 48 h and 72 h post-transfection.
- Majzner 2022 / Monje 2025 use a closely-related MSCV-promoter retroviral CAR with an upstream iCasp9, transduced on the CliniMACS Prodigy.

### 1.3 Envelope choices

- **GALV** (PG13 line): default for SFG-based CARs (Pule 2008, Heczey 2017). Compatible with CD8+/CD4+ human T cells via the PiT-1 receptor.
- **RD114**: used by Mount 2018 (PMID 29662203) and many Stanford constructs; transient packaging.
- **VSV-G** (rare for retrovirus due to cytotoxicity to producer cells): not the default.

### 1.4 Transient vs stable producer

- Stable producer (PG13 master cell bank) is the GMP-grade norm for Baylor and Bambino Gesù clinical products (Heczey 2017 "the producer cell clone was validated under Good Manufacturing Practice guidelines").
- Transient transfection (Phoenix or 293GP) is the standard preclinical approach (Pule 2008, Long 2015, Mount 2018).

## 2. Lentiviral platforms

### 2.1 Standard 3rd-generation HIV-1 lentivirus, VSV-G pseudotyped

- Increasingly the default for newer GD2 CARs because of (a) larger payload capacity, (b) ability to transduce non-dividing cells, (c) more straightforward GMP supply via contract manufacturers, and (d) a perceived safer integration profile.
- Glienke 2022 (PMID 35401506) provides a particularly detailed GMP example: a 3rd-generation self-inactivating (SIN) lentivirus carrying an "all-in-one" cassette (constitutive 14g2a-4-1BB-CD3ζ CAR + NFAT-responsive IL-18) is produced by transient triple-plasmid transfection (transfer plasmid + pCMVΔR8.74 packaging + pMD.G envelope + pcDNA3.HIV-Tat helper) in 293T cells in the presence of 25 µM chloroquine. Viral particles are concentrated and titered on HT1080 fibroblasts via spinoculation-mediated transduction (Methods, lines 156–175).
- Richman 2018 (PMID 29180536) places the GD2-E101K and m3F8 scFvs into a lentiviral vector under EF1α with CD8α hinge/TM and 4-1BB-CD3ζ ICDs.

### 2.2 SIN modifications

- Self-inactivating LTRs in the 3' U3 region are universal in modern clinical lentiviruses to prevent insertional activation of host proto-oncogenes by the LTR.

### 2.3 Insulators and chromatin context

- Some 2023–2026 papers in the collection report insulator elements (cHS4-derived) flanking the cassette to homogenize CAR expression across integrants and reduce silencing.

## 3. Non-viral DNA delivery

### 3.1 Sleeping Beauty and piggyBac transposition

- Several preclinical GD2 CAR papers in the collection use SB100x and piggyBac transposases co-electroporated with plasmid-borne CAR cassettes. Lower integration copy number, but transposition occurs into TA dinucleotides (SB) or TTAA (piggyBac) preferentially in active chromatin. Used to avoid retroviral / lentiviral GMP supply chains.

### 3.2 Minicircle and nanoplasmid

- Cappabianca 2024 (PMID 38699288, Methods) explicitly uses **Nanoplasmid HDR donor template vectors** (Aldevron) and **Nanoplasmid transposon vectors** to deliver an anti-GD2 CAR into the TRAC locus by CRISPR-Cas9 HDR. Nanoplasmids have a minimal RNA-only backbone (no bacterial ori/marker in the DNA payload region), giving improved expression and reduced sensing.
- Balke-Want 2023 (PMID 37365642) uses nanoplasmid DNA + a HITI (homology-independent targeted insertion) strategy: Cas9 RNP plus nanoplasmid encoding the CAR flanked by cut sites recognized by the same Cas9 guide. Inside the cell the donor is liberated as a free dsDNA fragment with overhanging Cas9 cuts that integrate into the TRAC locus via NHEJ.

### 3.3 In-vitro-transcribed (IVT) mRNA + electroporation

- Singh 2014 (PMID 25104548) and several follow-ups in the collection generate **transient** GD2 CAR T cells by electroporating IVT mRNA encoding the CAR. CAR expression decays over 7–10 days; useful for repeat-dosing strategies that limit on-target/off-tumor exposure.
- Recent (2024–2026) papers in the collection deliver CAR mRNA in **lipid nanoparticles (LNPs)** for in-vivo CAR programming, avoiding ex vivo manufacturing entirely.

## 4. CRISPR-Cas9 / TALEN site-directed integration

### 4.1 AAV6-HDR

- Mueller 2022 (PMID 36382633): a "virus-free CRISPR CAR" 9-day manufacturing process. Cas9 RNP + dsDNA donor delivered by electroporation insert an anti-GD2 CAR into the TRAC locus. Yields TRAC-KO + CAR-KI in a single workflow.
- Earlier MacLeod / Eyquem-style AAV6-HDR strategies (referenced by Mueller 2022 and Balke-Want 2023) use AAV6 as the donor carrier. AAV6 requires its own GMP supply, which Mueller 2022 set out to eliminate.

### 4.2 HITI (homology-independent targeted insertion)

- Balke-Want 2023 (PMID 37365642): nanoplasmid DNA, CRISPR-Cas9 RNP and HITI yield 2× more knock-in CAR T cells than HDR. Combined with **CEMENT** (CRISPR EnrichMENT, methotrexate-selected DHFR^L22F/F31S marker), the process scales to 5.5 × 10^8 – 3.6 × 10^9 CAR+ T cells in a 14-day GMP-compatible run.

### 4.3 Endogenous TRAC promoter-driven CAR

- Mueller 2022 and Balke-Want 2023 both place the GD2 CAR cassette under the **endogenous TRAC promoter**, simultaneously knocking out the TCR (preventing GvHD in allogeneic products) and giving more physiological CAR expression that is dynamically modulated by TCR signaling pathways.

### 4.4 PD-1, B2M, CIITA edits

- Multi-knockout allogeneic GD2 CAR-T platforms in the collection (Quintarelli 2025 supplement PMID 39815015; 2025–2026 preclinical papers) use TALEN or Cas9 to additionally knock out PDCD1 (PD-1) for resistance to PD-L1 inhibition, β2-microglobulin (B2M) to reduce MHC I recognition, and CIITA to reduce MHC II antigen presentation in donor-derived products.

## 5. Transduction conditions

### 5.1 Spinoculation on RetroNectin-coated plates

- Universal across the field for primary T cells. Typical conditions (Pule 2008 / Heczey 2017 / Quintarelli 2018 / Mount 2018 / Majzner 2022):
  - 24-well non-tissue-culture-treated plates pre-coated with 4–10 µg/mL recombinant human RetroNectin (Takara CH-296).
  - 1–2 mL of retroviral supernatant per well.
  - Spinoculation at ~1000–2000 ×g for 60–90 min at 32 °C.
  - 1–2 rounds of transduction on day 3–4 post-activation.
- Spinoculation efficiency: typically 35–80% CAR+ for retrovirus, 20–50% for lentivirus.

### 5.2 TransAct / Dynabead activation prior to lentiviral transduction

- Glienke 2022 (PMID 35401506): CD3/CD28 MACS GMP T Cell **TransAct** beads (Miltenyi) for 72 h activation, then lentiviral spinoculation on day 1 with 10 mL viral supernatant in 100 mL final volume on the Prodigy.
- Modern closed-system manufacturing (Lonza Cocoon, Miltenyi Prodigy) integrates spinoculation into automated agitation phases.

### 5.3 Electroporation for non-viral knock-in

- Cappabianca 2024 (PMID 38699288) uses the Lonza 4D-Nucleofector or LV LE Nucleofector at clinical scale. Electroporation conditions vary by platform; Mueller 2022 describes settings that preserve >85% viability after RNP + dsDNA donor delivery.

### 5.4 Polycations and small molecules

- Some papers add **protamine sulfate** (8 µg/mL) to viral supernatants instead of polybrene to improve transduction without cytotoxicity (Glienke 2022 PMID 35401506).
- Spinoculation supplemented with **rapamycin** or **dasatinib** during activation is the Stanford Mackall lab approach to enrich for stem-cell-memory T cells during transduction.

## 6. Practical comparison table

| Platform | Representative GD2 CAR papers | Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SFG / MoMLV gamma retrovirus | Pule 2008, Louis 2011, Heczey 2014/2017, Tian 2025 | Mature GMP stable producer banks (PG13); 35–80% transduction | Requires dividing T cells; smaller payload; intellectual-property history |
| MSGV / MSCV gamma retrovirus | Long 2015, Mount 2018, Majzner 2022, Monje 2025 | Higher transgene expression | Same constraints as SFG |
| 3G SIN lentivirus | Richman 2018, Glienke 2022, Cocoon-based clinical programs | Larger payload, safer integration, ability to carry inducible armoring (IL-18) | More complex 4-plasmid GMP production |
| SB / piggyBac transposon | preclinical-only papers | Cheap, no virus | Random integration; copy number variability |
| AAV6-HDR knock-in | early TRAC-CAR studies | Site-directed; physiological CAR expression | Still requires AAV6 GMP |
| Virus-free CRISPR knock-in (RNP + nanoplasmid) | Mueller 2022 PMID 36382633, Balke-Want 2023, Cappabianca 2024 | No viral supply chain; TRAC-driven CAR; simultaneous TCR-KO | Lower KI efficiency without selection (HITI/CEMENT helps) |
| In-situ mRNA-LNP | recent 2025–2026 papers | No ex-vivo manufacturing; redoseable | Transient CAR expression; biodistribution challenges |
