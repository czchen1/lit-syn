# Clinical formulation: lymphodepletion, dose, route, redosing

## 1. Lymphodepleting chemotherapy (LD)

LD before CAR-T infusion ablates host lymphocytes to create homeostatic cytokine support (IL-7, IL-15) and eliminate competing T cells. Across the 14 GD2 CAR-T clinical trials in the collection, the dominant regimen is **cyclophosphamide + fludarabine** (Cy/Flu) with minor variations.

### 1.1 Standard Cy/Flu

- Heczey 2017 (PMID 28602436): cyclophosphamide 500 mg/m²/day on days −4, −3, −2 + fludarabine 30 mg/m²/day on days −4 and −3. Cohort 1 received no Cy/Flu; cohorts 2 and 3 received Cy/Flu, which significantly enhanced CAR T expansion.
- Majzner 2022 (PMID 35130560) standard IV dose: cyclophosphamide 500 mg/m²/day + fludarabine 25 mg/m²/day on days −4, −3, −2.
- Majzner 2022 alternate (more aggressive) for the first ICV patient (spinal DMG-1): cyclophosphamide 600 mg/m²/day + fludarabine 30 mg/m²/day on days −5, −4, −3, −2.
- Monje 2025 (PMID 39537919): same Cy/Flu LD for IV doses; subsequent ICV infusions did not require LD.
- Del Bufalo 2023 NEJM GD2-CART01: Cy/Flu at conventional doses; full details in supplement.
- Locatelli 2025 (PMID 40926103): Cy/Flu for GD2-CART01 follow-on phase 1/2 in NB.

### 1.2 LD as a manufacturing/biology trigger

- Heczey 2017 (PMID 28602436) showed Cy/Flu LD increases peripheral IL-15 levels (mean 43 pg/mL vs 11 pg/mL in unconditioned cohort) and correlates with CAR-T expansion (Spearman r = 0.796, p = 0.003).

### 1.3 No-LD strategies

- The first cohort of Pule 2008 (PMID 18978797) did not receive LD; CAR-CTLs persisted only weeks. This was the empirical basis for adding LD in later trials.

## 2. Routes of administration

### 2.1 Intravenous (IV)

- Default for systemic NB (Pule 2008, Heczey 2017, Heczey 2020/2023, Del Bufalo 2023, Locatelli 2025, Tian 2025, Gargett 2024).
- Typical infusion: 50–100 mL volume over 10–30 min, premedication with chlorpheniramine + ondansetron (Quintarelli 2025 supplement).

### 2.2 Intracerebroventricular (ICV) via Ommaya reservoir

- Majzner 2022 (PMID 35130560) and Monje 2025 (PMID 39537919) — DIPG/DMG. Patients with DIPG required mandatory Ommaya reservoir placement. Initial IV dose at DL1/DL2, followed by repeated ICV infusions of 10–30×10⁶ GD2-CAR-T in 3 mL volume; no LD required for ICV doses.
- 41 of 62 ICV infusions in Monje 2025 had no CRS; the remaining 19 had mostly grade 1–2 CRS.
- ICV delivery markedly improved drug exposure in CSF and clinical activity in DMG.

### 2.3 Intratumoral

- A small set of preclinical papers and one clinical sarcoma trial in the collection deliver GD2 CAR-T intratumorally (intraosseous for bone tumors).

### 2.4 Locoregional (peritoneal, intrapleural)

- A handful of 2024–2026 preclinical papers explore intraperitoneal or intrapleural GD2 CAR-T for peritoneal carcinomatosis-type GD2+ tumors.

### 3.2 Fractionated / split dosing

- Majzner 2022 and Monje 2025: explicit fractionated ICV redosing (typically every 3–4 weeks) once IV CAR levels and toxicity have resolved (CAR < 5% of lymphocytes, no active toxicity, ≥ 28 days from prior infusion).

## 4. Redosing

- The Stanford DMG protocol allows up to 12 ICV redoses; 7 of 20 successful manufacturing runs in Monje 2025 were re-manufactured products for repeat ICV infusion in the same patient.
- The Bambino Gesù NB protocols allow second IV infusion after initial response and toxicity resolution.

## 5. Bridging therapy

- Most NB and DMG protocols permit (and explicitly recommend) bridging chemotherapy or radiotherapy during the manufacturing window (15–22 days from enrollment to first IV dose, per Monje 2025).

## 6. Toxicity management as a manufacturing-coupled decision

The product design (particularly the safety switch and the cytokine armoring choice) co-determines clinical management:

- iCasp9-equipped products (GD2-CART01, GD2-CAR3, GD2-CAR DMG): protocols allow rimiducid activation as a rescue maneuver. Not yet activated in published trials.
- IL-15-armored CAR-NKT products (Heczey 2020/2023; Tian 2025): protocols include monitoring for hyperleukocytosis at higher dose levels, with consideration of leukapheresis or rimiducid (if construct includes iCasp9) as rescue.
- ICV-administered DMG products (Majzner 2022; Monje 2025): protocols mandate routine and symptom-prompted ICP monitoring, mandatory Ommaya, prophylactic tocilizumab/anakinra for grade ≥ 2 CRS, and corticosteroids only after anti-cytokine therapy fails — to preserve CAR-T function.

## 7. Concomitant medications

- Premedication: paracetamol, diphenhydramine, methylprednisolone (low dose) before infusion in most NB trials.
- Anti-cytokine: tocilizumab (IL-6R antagonist), anakinra (IL-1R antagonist) used preemptively or reactively for CRS / TIAN.
- Corticosteroids: deferred where possible; high-dose only for grade ≥ 3 neurotoxicity unresponsive to anti-cytokine.

## 8. Cellular kinetics correlation with formulation

- Cy/Flu LD strongly correlates with peak peripheral CAR copies/µg DNA, peak IL-15 levels, and ultimate clinical response (Heczey 2017 PMID 28602436).
- IV peak persistence ~14–28 days; ICV delivery increases CSF CAR detection while reducing systemic peak (Monje 2025).
