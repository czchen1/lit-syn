# Focused ultrasound-mediated BBB opening (FUS-BBBO)

FUS with intravenous microbubbles transiently and reversibly opens the BBB at a
targeted focus. For the pons it is attractive because it is non-invasive, repeatable,
and can be aimed at a volume rather than a catheter track — but the posterior fossa
imposes a harder acoustic problem (skull incidence, transducer geometry) than the
supratentorial targets where FUS clinical practice matured.

## Brainstem-specific preclinical evidence

- Alli 2018 (PMID 29753957) demonstrated brainstem BBB disruption with FUS and
  enhanced doxorubicin delivery — the feasibility anchor for the pons.
- Englander 2021 (PMID 33753753) and Ishida 2021 (PMID 33188825) established safety and
  drug-delivery enhancement in murine pontine glioma / experimental DIPG models.
- Payload studies: panobinostat (Martinez 2023, PMID 37795179), olaparib as a
  radiosensitiser ('t Hart 2023, PMID 37019285), liposomal payloads (Payne 2024,
  bimodal confirmation of liposome delivery after BBBO).
- Combination with radiotherapy: Tazhibi 2024 (PMID 38555449) showed FUS-BBBO is safe
  and feasible with moderately hypofractionated radiotherapy in brainstem DMG models.
- Sonication strategy is a design variable: Gong 2022 (PMID 35476579) compared
  sonication patterns and microbubble administration strategies for large-volume
  delivery — relevant because a pontine target needs volumetric, not point, opening.
- FUS also enhances other routes: Ye 2020 (PMID 32871204) characterised
  FUS-mediated *brainstem* delivery of intranasally administered agents (FUSIN),
  combining a BBB-bypassing route with focal targeting.

## Clinical translation

- Wu 2025 (PMID 41223245) reported BBB opening with neuronavigation-guided FUS in
  pediatric patients with diffuse midline glioma — the first-line clinical evidence
  that pontine/midline BBBO is achievable in children.
- Consensus and programme-level reviews frame the agenda: Parekh 2023 (PMID 36709715,
  FUS Foundation DIPG/DMG consensus), Chesney 2024 (PMID 38702518), Martinez 2024
  (PMID 38000663), Lu 2025 (PMID 39941880, trials of FUS for brain tumours).
- Device evolution is relevant to the pons: skull-implanted ultrasound arrays
  (Habashy 2026) remove the skull as an attenuator and enable repeated sessions;
  transcranial MRgFUS engineering reviews (Kamimura 2025) cover targeting and
  thermal-prediction constraints.

## Secondary uses that matter for pontine trials

FUS is not only a delivery tool. Zhang 2026 (PMID 42281802) used FUS to enrich
circulating tumour biomarkers in a DMG mouse model — a non-invasive "liquid biopsy
amplifier" for a tumour that is difficult to sample. Sonodynamic therapy (Hutton 2024,
PMID 39217635; PpIX-based approaches, Marcus 2024, PMID 38398131) uses the same
hardware for a cytotoxic rather than a delivery endpoint.

## Open issues

Dose–response of opening (how much albumin/contrast extravasation equals therapeutic
concentration) is rarely measured in the pons; the safety envelope for repeated opening
adjacent to respiratory nuclei is not established; and BBBO does nothing about efflux
once the drug is inside, which is why FUS+efflux-aware chemistry and FUS+nanocarrier
combinations recur in this literature.
