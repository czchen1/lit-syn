# Nanocarriers and the intranasal route

Both of these are best understood as *modifiers* of the routes in the other notes rather
than standalone pontine delivery solutions: a nanocarrier changes residence time,
solubility and uptake of whatever route carries it, and the intranasal route provides a
non-invasive entry point that is then focused by ultrasound or by cellular homing.

## Nanocarriers in DIPG/DMG

- **Formulation solves solubility, not location.** MTX110 (water-soluble panobinostat)
  and nanoliposomal SN-38 exist because the free drugs cannot be delivered at the
  required local concentration; both are used with a route (CED for MTX110, intranasal
  for nanoliposomal SN-38, Sasaki 2023, PMID 36599085 — see below).
  Nano-micellar formulation can also be the enabler of a route: Singleton 2017
  (PMID 28260886) prolonged survival with CED of panobinostat-loaded pluronic
  nano-micelles in rat brainstem glioma.
- **Carrier as depot for CED.** Bellat 2020 (PMID 32301996) combined CED with a
  peptide-nanofiber reservoir to prolong local DM1 retention — the clearest example of
  nanotechnology addressing the residence-time failure mode.
- **Carrier as payload for FUS.** Payne 2024 (PMID 39624326) confirmed liposome delivery to brain after
  FUS-BBBO with two independent modalities, defining the time window in which large
  carriers can cross.
- **Targeted and gene-silencing carriers in DIPG models.** Functionalised macrophage
  exosomes co-delivering panobinostat and PPM1D-siRNA (Shan 2022, PMID 35585670),
  albumin-functionalised gold nanoparticles for doxorubicin
  (Ung 2020 abstract), liposomal nanocarriers for targeting and optical imaging of
  brainstem tumours (Kambhampati 2014 abstract), spherical nucleic acids against IDO1
  (Lenzen 2017 abstract), and myeloid-reprogramming nanoparticle resiquimod
  (McSwain 2026).
- **Reviews specific to the disease**: Bredlau 2017 (PMID 26903150, nanotechnology
  applications for DIPG), Presswala 2024 (PMID 39091904), Mohamed 2026 (PMID 42307690,
  DMG nanoparticle prospects), Alaseem 2025 (PMID 40828479, BBB-crossing strategies in
  pediatric CNS tumours).
- **Radiation interaction.** Metallic nanoparticles as radiosensitisers with
  distance-dependent Auger effects in DMG (Al-Husseini 2026, PMID 42394610) and local delivery of
  radiosensitising nanoparticles to pediatric brainstem gliomas (2016 abstract) tie the
  nanocarrier literature to the radiotherapy backbone that every DIPG patient receives.
- **Extracellular vesicles cut both ways**: they are candidate carriers, but in DMG they
  also mediate radioresistance signalling (Oza 2025, PMID 41167985; Morcos 2026,
  PMID 42352467), so an EV-based
  delivery platform is not biologically neutral.

Caveat: the majority of nanocarrier records in this collection are preclinical or
platform reviews. No nanocarrier has a completed pontine-specific clinical delivery
trial; where nanomedicine has reached DIPG patients it has done so *through* CED
(MTX110) or as a formulation of a conventional agent.

## Intranasal / nose-to-brain

- **DIPG-specific preclinical work.** Sasaki 2023 (PMID 36599085) — intranasal
  nanoliposomal SN-38 for DMG (preceded by He 2017/2018 abstracts on brainstem glioma).
  Chastkofsky 2017 (abstracts, and 2021 full paper for the MSC/virus combination) —
  intranasal MSCs home to DIPG in mouse models.
- **Focusing the route.** Ye 2020 (PMID 32871204) and related FUSIN work combine
  intranasal administration with focused ultrasound to concentrate agents in the
  brainstem — the intranasal route supplies drug to the CNS broadly, and FUS supplies
  the spatial selectivity the nose cannot.
- **Transferable formulation science** comes largely from neurodegeneration: chitosan and
  lipid nanocarriers, nasal deposition targeting the olfactory region, and hydrogel
  vehicles (see `10_related_diseases_lessons.md`). Clinical evidence for nose-to-brain
  delivery in humans remains limited; the DIPG-relevant records are all animal studies.

## Judgement

For the pons, nanocarriers are most credible where they extend local residence after a
direct infusion, or where they are the only way to formulate an otherwise undeliverable
agent. Intranasal delivery is attractive for repeated, non-invasive dosing in children
but currently lacks quantitative pontine exposure data in humans; the FUS-assisted
variant is the most plausible clinical path.
