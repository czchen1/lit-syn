# Intraventricular, intrathecal and other intra-CSF routes

The CSF route trades parenchymal penetration for compartment coverage. Drug given into
the ventricles or thecal sac reaches CSF-adjacent disease and the leptomeninges at high
concentration but penetrates only millimetres into solid parenchyma. For DIPG this makes
it complementary to CED/FUS: the pons itself is a parenchymal target, whereas the
subventricular and leptomeningeal spread seen in a large fraction of cases is a CSF
target.

## Direct fourth-ventricle infusion

The most pons-adjacent CSF strategy is infusion into the fourth ventricle, developed in
large-animal models by Sandberg's group:

- Sandberg 2008 (PMID 18447671) — chemotherapy directly into the fourth ventricle in a
  new piglet model.
- Sandberg 2012 (PMID 22546032) — the same approach in a nonhuman primate model.

These studies define tolerability of repeated dosing into a ventricle bounded by the
dorsal pons and medulla, and they are the translational bridge for administering agents
to the surface of a pontine tumour without traversing it.

## Access hardware and its failure modes

- Reservoirs/ports: Sandberg 2000 (PMID 10917346) on Ommaya placement technique and
  complication avoidance; Zairi 2011 (PMID 21107993) on complications of intraventricular
  chemotherapy devices; Dupoiron 2023 on intrathecal catheter + subcutaneous port.
- Route comparisons: Zhang 2026 (PMID 42234048) meta-analysed Ommaya reservoir versus
  lumbar puncture for intrathecal chemotherapy; Jiang 2026 (PMID 42239892) reviewed
  Ommaya-based intrathecal therapy in leptomeningeal metastasis.
- Administration technique changes distribution: Tuohy 2025 (PMID 41080198) showed that
  the *technique* of intra-Ommaya administration alters CSF drug distribution — a
  reminder that "intraventricular" is not a single, reproducible exposure.
- Programmable VP shunts as access devices: McThenia 2022 (PMID 35403968).
- CSF flow patency must be verified first: Patel 2018 (PMID 29170836) used
  phase-contrast CSF flow MRI to check pathway patency before intra-CSF therapy —
  directly relevant to pontine/posterior-fossa tumours that obstruct the fourth
  ventricle.
- Human CSF tracer distribution in the upper cord and brainstem is now measurable
  (Melin 2023, PMID 38063195), providing the pharmacokinetic map this route lacked.

## Payloads delivered intra-CSF in CNS tumours

Topotecan (intraventricular, 50 pediatric patients, DDEL-05 2022 abstract), CDK4/6
inhibitors given metronomically intrathecally in pediatric brain tumour models
(MODL-05 2022 abstract), nanoparticle-formulated panobinostat for intrathecal use
(Chaudhuri 2020 abstract), radioimmunotherapy with omburtamab, and — most importantly
for DMG — CAR-T cells (see `05_cell_and_viral_vector_delivery.md`).

## Non-oncologic CSF delivery experience worth importing

The neurogenetics field has industrialised this route and its lessons transfer directly:
intrathecal AAV9 gene therapy in CLN7 (Greenberg 2026, PMID 41314141), intrathecal
onasemnogene abeparvovec in SMA (Kwon 2026, PMID 41360995), intrathecal idursulfase-IT
in MPS II (Muenzer 2026, PMID 41737901), long-term intraventricular enzyme replacement
in CLN2 including the practical shift from ventricular access device to chest-sited port
(Gopalka 2026, PMID 41897078), and nurse-led intrathecal tofersen clinics in ALS
(Jewett 2026, PMID 42438447). Together they show that repeated, protocolised CSF dosing
in children is operationally routine — the constraint in DIPG is the drug and the
parenchymal barrier, not the access.

Device infection, CSF leak and immunogenicity are the recurring complications in that
literature rather than parenchymal toxicity, and glymphatic/perivascular
transport work is included where it explains how CSF-borne agents move into brainstem
tissue.
