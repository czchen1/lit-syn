# Convection-enhanced delivery (CED) to the brainstem

CED is the only pontine delivery route with repeated, completed, prospective clinical
experience in DIPG. A cannula placed in the pons infuses at low flow rates so that
bulk flow (not diffusion) distributes the agent through the interstitium, making the
BBB irrelevant to the delivery step.

## Preclinical foundation

- **Feasibility and tolerance in the brainstem.** Sewing 2014 (PMID 25263805)
  established murine brainstem CED of carmustine with acceptable tolerance and a
  survival signal. Thomale 2009 built a neurological scoring system for the rat
  brainstem glioma model precisely because functional morbidity, not just survival,
  determines whether a pontine infusion is acceptable.
- **Infusate parameters matter as much as the drug.** Rechberger 2020 (PMID 31896090)
  compared infusate parameters for direct brainstem delivery, and Sandberg 2002
  (PMID 12187954) showed hyperosmolar mannitol alters CED distribution in rat
  brainstem — i.e. the tissue's hydraulic properties are a tunable variable.
- **Agent classes delivered by brainstem CED** in models include EZH2 inhibitors
  (Sasaki 2020, PMID 32674144), PI3K/MEK combinations (Chang 2019, PMID 32642647),
  dasatinib with efflux blockade (Tsvankin 2020, PMID 31225627), pan-RAS inhibition
  (Sbergio 2026, PMID 42371788), and radioimmunoconjugates (Luther 2014,
  PMID 24526309, theragnostic ¹²⁴I-8H9).
- **Toxicology of the infused agent in brainstem tissue is a separate question from
  systemic toxicology.** Singleton 2018 (PMID 29856296) characterised distribution,
  clearance and brainstem toxicity of CED panobinostat in juvenile rat and pig, and
  Singleton 2017 (PMID 28260886) showed pluronic nano-micellar panobinostat delivered by
  CED prolonged survival in rat brainstem glioma — the preclinical package behind
  MTX110 trials.
- **Residence time is the recurring failure mode.** Power 2023 (PMID 37128506) argues
  explicitly that increasing drug–tumour residence time, not merely getting the drug
  in, is the translational barrier; Bellat 2020 (PMID 32301996) answered this with a
  CED-delivered peptide-nanofiber reservoir prolonging local DM1 retention.

## Clinical experience in DIPG

| Study | Agent | Design | Key point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anderson 2013 (PMID 23240851) | topotecan | children, brainstem CED | early demonstration of intratumoural infusion in diffuse brainstem tumours |
| Souweidane 2018 (PMID 29914796) | ¹²⁴I-8H9 (omburtamab) | single-centre phase 1 dose escalation | CED feasible; lesion dosimetry measurable by PET |
| Heiss 2019 (PMID 30544335) | IL13-Pseudomonas exotoxin | phase 1 | targeted toxin delivered by CED in children with DIPG |
| Szychot 2021 (PMID 33575829) | carboplatin + sodium valproate | intermittent CED into the pons | repeat/intermittent infusion via implanted catheters, addressing residence time |
| Mueller 2023 (PMID 37318058) | MTX110 (aqueous panobinostat) | PNOC015 phase 1, repeated CED | repeated dosing through an implanted catheter; drug chosen for CED compatibility |
| Saito 2025 (PMID 40150841) | nimustine (ACNU) | multicentre phase 2 | largest prospective CED experience in children with DIPG |

## Safety, imaging and mechanics specific to the pons

- Hollingworth 2022 (PMID 35933568) documented infusion-related side-effects during
  brainstem CED — transient, dose/volume-related neurological events are expected and
  must be planned for, not treated as protocol failures.
- Bander 2020 (PMID 31896089) measured deformational change in the pediatric brainstem
  after CED; infused volume physically displaces tissue.
- Distribution must be imaged, otherwise "delivered" is an assumption. MR-visible
  surrogates (Saito 2005, PMID 16197944; Krauze 2005, PMID 16181805 — including
  brainstem targets in non-human primates) and the DIPG-specific CED imaging protocol
  work from the UCL/GOSH programme (DIPG-18, 2022 abstract) provide the tooling.

## What CED does not solve

Focal coverage. Infusion covers a volume of distribution around the catheter, while
DIPG infiltrates beyond it and disseminates to leptomeningeal/subventricular
compartments in a large minority of cases. CED therefore pairs naturally with
CSF-directed or systemic strategies rather than replacing them.
