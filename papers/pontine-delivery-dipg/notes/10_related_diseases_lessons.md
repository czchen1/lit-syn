# Lessons from related diseases

These records were kept only where a *direct* delivery route (CED/parenchymal infusion,
CSF, focused ultrasound, intra-arterial, implanted device) was used in humans or large
animals for a non-DIPG disease, because the operational knowledge transfers even when the
biology does not.

## Chronic CSF dosing in children is an established practice

- **CLN2 (cerliponase alfa)** is the strongest precedent: ICV enzyme replacement in
  children via an implanted reservoir, with dose selection translated from animal models
  (Hammon 2021, PMID 34076336), immunogenicity monitoring over repeated dosing
  (Cherukuri 2018, PMID 30205177), long-term real-world practice (de los Reyes 2020,
  PMID 32684372; Soangra 2025, PMID 39762319) and hardware evolution from ventricular
  access device to chest-sited port (Gopalka 2026, PMID 41897078).
- **MPS II / Sanfilippo** replicate the pattern with idursulfase-beta and tralesinidase
  alfa (Sohn 2018, PMID 29978271; Seo 2021, PMID 33768130; Seo 2023, PMID 37922836;
  Pinkstaff 2023, PMID 36923444; Ueda 2026, PMID 42496452), including same-day combined
  intravitreal + ICV dosing (Rogers 2025, PMID 40578219).
- **SMA and CLN7 gene therapy**: intrathecal onasemnogene (Kwon 2026, PMID 41360995) and
  first-in-human high-dose intrathecal AAV9 for CLN7 (Greenberg 2026, PMID 41314141) show
  intrathecal vector dosing at clinical scale, with implantable intrathecal ports now in
  trials.

Transferable lessons: reservoirs/ports can be maintained for years in children; infection
and device revision are the main recurring complications; immunogenicity and anti-vector
immunity limit repeat dosing; and CSF concentration is not a reliable proxy for
parenchymal exposure.

## Direct parenchymal infusion outside oncology

- **Neuronopathic Gaucher disease**: Lonser 2005 (PMID 15786474) and Lonser 2007
  (PMID 17065591) — convection perfusion and then image-guided direct convective
  delivery of glucocerebrosidase, the same technique/imaging package as tumour CED but
  for an enzyme; Meng 2022 (PMID 36089809) later delivered recombinant glucocerebrosidase
  to the putamen with MR-guided focused ultrasound, i.e. the same payload by two
  different routes.
- **AADC deficiency and Parkinson disease**: MR-guided intraputaminal AAV2-AADC
  (Pearson 2021, PMID 34253733), and the AAV2-hAADC comparison of intraputaminal versus ICV delivery
  (Compton 2023, PMID 36472076) — a controlled route comparison rarely available in
  oncology.
- These programmes established intraoperative MRI infusion monitoring, real-time
  distribution feedback and cannula designs that pontine CED subsequently reused.

## Focused ultrasound matured in adult neurology first

FUS BBB opening in Parkinson and Alzheimer disease supplied the safety framework,
implanted-array hardware and acoustic monitoring standards later applied to DMG; the
posterior fossa remains the harder target because of skull geometry and the absence of a
tolerance margin for oedema.

## What does not transfer

- Non-oncologic targets are usually diffuse (whole cortex, whole cord), so wide, low
  concentration CSF distribution is acceptable — the opposite of the focal,
  high-concentration requirement in a pontine tumour.
- Enzyme and ASO products are non-cytotoxic, so extravasation or misdistribution is far
  less dangerous than with chemotherapy or a replicating virus.
- Treatment horizons are years, allowing device strategies that a 9–12 month median
  survival cannot justify.
