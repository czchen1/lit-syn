# Locoregional cellular therapy and viral vectors

For cells and viruses the delivery question is different from small molecules: the agent
replicates, migrates and traffics, so the route determines the *starting* distribution
and the systemic toxicity profile rather than the whole exposure.

## CAR-T: the route moved intracranial

The clearest recent shift in DMG therapeutics is from intravenous to locoregional
(intratumoural / intracerebroventricular, ICV) CAR-T administration via an indwelling
ventricular catheter and reservoir.

- **B7-H3, ICV (BrainChild-03, Seattle Children's).** Vitanza 2025 (PMID 39775044) is
  the completed phase 1 of ICV B7-H3 CAR-T for DIPG; Ronsley 2026 (PMID 42503899)
  extends the same delivery platform to non-pontine DMG and other recurrent pediatric
  CNS tumours. Ronsley 2026 (PMID 41798119) describes tumour inflammation-associated
  neurotoxicity in DIPG patients receiving B7-H3 CAR-T — the pons-specific toxicity that
  locoregional delivery creates, since local effector expansion happens inside a
  brainstem with no room.
- **GD2, IV then ICV (Stanford).** Monje 2025 (PMID 39537919) reports intravenous plus
  intracranial GD2-CAR T cells for H3K27M+ DMG; Jiang 2026 (PMID 40682569) reports a
  phase 1 GD2-CAR trial with mechanistic analysis in H3K27M+ DIPG. The IV-then-ICV
  sequence is itself a delivery design: systemic priming, then compartment dosing.
- **Other locoregional programmes.** Gust 2025 (PMID 40070357) reports locoregional
  EGFR806-CAR T infusion for recurrent/refractory pediatric CNS tumours (BrainChild-01).
- **Making delivered cells go where they are needed.** Vitanza 2025 (CXCR3-A engineering
  to enhance trafficking of ICV-delivered B7-H3 CAR-T) and Timpanaro 2026
  (PMID 41639009, B7-H3 CAR-T + ONC206 in DIPG) treat trafficking and combination as
  delivery-adjacent problems. Chen 2026 shows anti-CAR immunity driving acquired
  resistance to GD2-CAR — repeat dosing through a port has an immunological ceiling.
- **Measuring where cells went.** Li 2026 (PMID 42068336) uses dual-modality imaging for
  longitudinal biodistribution of ICV CAR-T — the cellular analogue of gadolinium
  co-infusion in CED.
- **Route can be arterial too.** Wang 2024 (PMID 38157532) enhanced CAR-T brain entry
  using intra-arterial NEO100, linking cell therapy to the intra-arterial literature.

## Cells as carriers

- Chastkofsky 2021 (PMID 33272983) — mesenchymal stem cells deliver oncolytic
  virotherapy to DIPG; the intranasal MSC route was reported earlier by the same group
  (2017 abstracts) as a non-invasive way to home carriers to the brainstem.
- Chai 2025 (PMID 41062177) — neural stem cell-delivered oncolytic virus given ICV in
  glioblastoma models, combining a CSF route with a cellular carrier.
- Carceller 2014 (PMID 24327128) — MSC-carried oncolytic adenovirus delivered by
  superselective intra-arterial catheterisation in DIPG.

## Oncolytic viruses and vectors in the pons

- **DNX-2401 (Delta-24-RGD).** Gállego Pérez-Larraya 2022 (PMID 35767439) — phase 1
  intratumoural DNX-2401 into the pons after biopsy, the landmark demonstration that
  a replicating virus can be injected into a DIPG with acceptable safety; reviewed for
  brainstem gliomas in Gállego Pérez-Larraya 2023 (PMID 37061388).
- **Ad-TD-nsIL12.** Qian 2025 (PMID 40721414) reports two phase 1 trials in primary and
  progressive pediatric IDH-wildtype DIPG (the adult HGG counterpart is Ning 2024,
  PMID 39516192).
- **Combination with immune agonists** (Labiano 2025, CD40 costimulation with
  Delta-24-RGD in DMG) and with engineered T cells (Vazaios 2025, γδ-TCR T cells plus
  oncolytic virus) are the current direction; these change what the injected volume
  achieves rather than how it is delivered.
- **AAV vectors** matter mainly as a route-comparison resource: whole-brain
  biodistribution after ICV injection across serotypes (Ye 2026, PMID 42518515),
  intrathecal AAVrh10 CNS gene transfer (Hordeaux 2015, PMID 25588740), and the
  intraputaminal-versus-ICV comparison for AAV2-hAADC (Compton 2023, PMID 36472076) —
  direct evidence that parenchymal infusion and CSF injection produce different
  distributions of the same vector. A reported neuroepithelial tumour with AAV
  integration after intracisternal magna delivery (Ahrens-Nicklas 2026) is the safety
  counterweight.

## Practical implications

1. An implanted ventricular catheter/reservoir turns a one-shot operation into a dosing
   platform; nearly all pontine cell-therapy progress depends on that hardware.
2. Locoregional dosing shifts toxicity from systemic CRS to local inflammation, which in
   the pons is the dose-limiting phenomenon.
3. Delivery success for cells is measured by trafficking and persistence, so imaging and
   CSF biomarker sampling belong in the delivery protocol.
