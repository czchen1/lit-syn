# Implants, depots, pumps and access devices

Two distinct ideas share this category:

1. **Depots** that release drug locally over time (wafers, hydrogels, microspheres,
   nanofiber reservoirs, osmotic pumps).
2. **Access hardware** that makes repeated delivery possible without repeated surgery
   (indwelling brainstem catheters with subcutaneous ports, ventricular reservoirs,
   programmable shunts, implanted ultrasound arrays, implanted intrathecal ports).

For the pons, only the second idea is currently clinical — there is no resection cavity
to line with a wafer — but the depot literature is the source of the residence-time
solutions the pontine field needs.

## Access hardware relevant to the pons

- Implanted catheter + port systems enabling *intermittent* CED into the pons
  (Szychot 2021, PMID 33575829; the Bristol/UCL intermittent-CED phase 1 abstracts,
  DDEL-07 2020 and SURG-07 2024) — the pontine analogue of an Ommaya reservoir.
- Ventricular reservoirs and programmable VP shunts as dosing platforms for ICV therapy
  (McThenia 2022, PMID 35403968; Zairi 2011, PMID 21107993; Sandberg 2000, PMID 10917346).
- Implantable intrathecal catheter-ports designed for chronic dosing, now being evaluated
  prospectively in SMA (ThecaFlex DRx, Mesina-Estarrón 2026, PMID 41830093) and adopted in CLN2
  (chest-sited port instead of a ventricular access device, Gopalka 2026, PMID 41897078).
- Skull-implanted ultrasound arrays for repeated BBB opening (Habashy 2026,
  PMID 42281806), which convert FUS from a session-based procedure into an implanted
  delivery device.
- Implantable intracerebral osmotic pumps for continuous convection-enhanced delivery
  (Berlet 2025, PMID 41132728), addressing the duration limit of a single infusion
  session.

## Depots and local-release matrices (mostly glioblastoma-derived)

- Carmustine (Gliadel) wafers remain the only FDA-approved implanted intracranial
  chemotherapy; the collection includes both effectiveness/complication series
  (Gkasdaris 2024, PMID 39456559; Houedjissin 2026, PMID 42187555; Roux 2025,
  PMID 40971079; Suryadevara 2025, PMID 41030714) and the complication
  literature that constrains any pontine analogue — CSF leak, eosinophilic meningitis,
  oedema and cyst formation (Kawaguchi 2024, PMID 38841004; Kuramitsu 2015,
  PMID 28663961). Kartal 2026 (PMID 41899511) reviews the limitations and
  next-generation directions.
- Injectable/in-situ hydrogels carrying nanoparticles, cells or immune agonists
  (Morshedi 2026, PMID 41116065; Maue 2026, PMID 41957222, thermoresponsive iNSC
  scaffold; Shang 2026, PMID 41499494, STING prodrug gel; Yadav 2025, PMID 41184309,
  conductive gels for electro-immunotherapy) show how a depot can
  co-deliver several agents at once, but all assume a cavity or an injectable volume that
  the pons cannot spare.
- Microneedle and other minimally invasive device concepts appear as reviews only; no
  brainstem application yet.
- CED-compatible depots are the crossover point: peptide nanofiber reservoirs
  (Bellat 2020, PMID 32301996), nano-micellar panobinostat (Singleton 2017,
  PMID 28260886), and self-assembling polypeptide-drug conjugates (DDEL-02 2024
  abstract for an IL13Rα2-targeted "self-depot" in GBM and DIPG).

## Design constraints a pontine device must satisfy

- Volume neutrality: displacement of even ~1 mL matters (Bander 2020, PMID 31896089).
- Catheter track must respect safe entry zones and long tracts (see
  `09_surgical_access_imaging_and_modeling.md`).
- Infection and CSF-leak risk over months of repeated access — the dominant complication
  class in both the Ommaya and Gliadel literature.
- Compatibility with MRI-based distribution monitoring and with radiotherapy planning,
  since almost all patients are irradiated.
