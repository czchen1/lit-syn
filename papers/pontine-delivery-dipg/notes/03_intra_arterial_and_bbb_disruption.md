# Intra-arterial delivery and osmotic BBB disruption

The idea is regional pharmacokinetics: inject into the artery that supplies the target
so first-pass concentration is high while systemic exposure stays lower, optionally
after opening the BBB with intra-arterial hyperosmolar mannitol. For the pons this
means the posterior circulation (basilar/vertebral, superselective pontine branches),
which is technically the least forgiving vascular territory in the brain.

## Brainstem-specific reports

- Fujiwara 1994 (PMID 8108006) — intra-arterial chemotherapy in four brainstem glioma
  cases; the earliest brainstem-specific series in this collection.
- Hall 2006 (PMID 16314949) — osmotic BBB disruption chemotherapy for diffuse pontine
  gliomas: the disease-specific attempt at barrier opening plus regional delivery.
- Riina 2010 (PMID 20377982) — balloon-assisted superselective intra-arterial infusion
  of bevacizumab for malignant brainstem glioma (technical note): flow arrest to
  maximise local residence in the posterior circulation.
- Carceller 2014 (PMID 24327128) — superselective intracerebral catheterisation to
  administer oncolytic virotherapy in DIPG; shows the route can carry biologics and
  cell-based carriers, not only small molecules.
- McCrea 2021 (PMID 34359048) — intra-arterial bevacizumab and cetuximab with BBB
  disruption in children with high-grade glioma and DIPG: the most substantial modern
  pediatric experience.

## Supporting adult/supratentorial and mechanistic evidence

- Barrier-opening physiology: Siegal 2000 (PMID 10761648) measured the *window* of
  barrier opening after osmotic disruption in humans — a delivery-timing parameter,
  not a binary state. Roman-Goldstein 1995 (PMID 7793380) paired MR findings with
  cognitive testing after osmotic disruption plus intra-arterial chemotherapy.
- Modern SIACI programmes: Boockvar 2011 (PMID 20964595, MTD of superselective
  intra-arterial bevacizumab after osmotic disruption), Chakraborty 2016
  (PMID 26945581, cetuximab), Patel 2021 (PMID 34601657, repeated SIACI bevacizumab),
  Uluc 2022 (PMID 35892048, safety of intra-arterial chemotherapy ± osmotic disruption),
  Ferreira 2026 (PMID 41043867 and PMID 40044413), and the systematic reviews by
  Huang 2020 (PMID 32983974) and Pinkiewicz 2022 (PMID 36212394).
- Pediatric non-brainstem CNS tumours: Jahnke 2008 (PMID 18072268) for embryonal/germ
  cell tumours, Osztie 2001 (PMID 11337321) for progressive optic-hypothalamic glioma —
  demonstrating tolerability of the approach in children.
- Emerging payloads: intra-arterial radioligand therapy (Sabuzi 2026, PMID 41594316)
  and biodegradable intra-arterial devices for focal delivery (Kinra 2026).
- CED interaction: Sandberg 2002 (PMID 12187954) showed hyperosmolar mannitol changes
  CED distribution in the rat brainstem, so osmotic manipulation is not independent of
  a concurrent parenchymal infusion strategy.

## Assessment for the pons

Intra-arterial delivery has the best *anatomical logic* for a diffusely infiltrating
pontine tumour — it perfuses the whole territory rather than one catheter's volume of
distribution — and the worst *risk profile*: posterior-circulation catheterisation,
non-selective opening of normal pontine barrier, and dependence on operator-level
neurointerventional expertise. Its current role in DIPG is exploratory, and the
literature is dominated by small series, technical notes and adult extrapolation. The
mechanistic parameters worth carrying forward are the opening window (Siegal),
flow-arrest/balloon assistance (Riina) and repeat-dose feasibility (Patel).
