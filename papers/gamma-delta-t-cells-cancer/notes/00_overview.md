# γδ T cells in cancer: overview

## Why γδ T cells are interesting for oncology

γδ T cells are a small, conserved lineage (typically 1–10% of circulating CD3⁺ cells, and a much larger fraction in epithelial and mucosal tissues) that bridge innate and adaptive immunity. Three properties make them attractive as a cancer therapeutic:

1. **MHC‑independent tumor recognition.** Unlike αβ T cells, γδ T cells do not require peptide presentation on classical HLA. They sense metabolic stress, stress‑induced surface ligands (e.g. MICA/B, ULBPs through NKG2D), and — for the blood‑dominant Vγ9Vδ2 subset — intracellular **phosphoantigen** accumulation reported to the cell surface by the butyrophilins **BTN3A1** and **BTN2A1**. This makes them broadly tumor‑reactive and, in principle, usable in an **allogeneic, off‑the‑shelf** setting without the graft‑versus‑host risk that HLA‑mismatched αβ T cells carry.
2. **Potent, multi‑modal effector function.** They kill via perforin/granzyme, death receptors (TRAIL, FasL), and antibody‑dependent cellular cytotoxicity (CD16/FcγRIII); secrete IFN‑γ and TNF‑α; and can act as professional antigen‑presenting cells.
3. **Favorable prognostic signal.** A pan‑cancer analysis of tumor‑infiltrating leukocyte signatures found that a γδ T cell signature was among the most favorable prognostic immune populations across many tumor types (Gentles et al., *Nat Med* 2015), galvanizing therapeutic interest — although this signal has since been substantially complicated (see below).

## The central tension: a double‑edged sword

The same plasticity that makes γδ T cells attractive also makes them dangerous. Depending on subset, cytokine milieu, and tumor type, γδ T cells can be strongly **antitumor** (IFN‑γ⁺, cytotoxic) or strongly **protumor** — most notably the **IL‑17‑producing γδ T17** cells that recruit immunosuppressive myeloid cells, drive angiogenesis, and promote metastasis. Much of the modern literature is an attempt to (a) understand what controls this fork and (b) engineer products that lock cells into the antitumor state. This collection is organized around that tension.

## Eras and inflection points

- **1980s–1990s — discovery and ligand mystery.** The γδ TCR is described; Vγ9Vδ2 cells are found to respond to non‑peptide "phosphoantigens" from microbes and from the host mevalonate pathway, but the presenting molecule remains unknown for two decades.
- **1994–2007 — aminobisphosphonate expansion and the first trials.** Aminobisphosphonates (e.g. **zoledronate**, pamidronate) are found to block farnesyl‑pyrophosphate synthase, causing intracellular **IPP** to accumulate and activate Vγ9Vδ2 cells. This enables large‑scale ex‑vivo expansion with zoledronate + IL‑2 and underpins the **first clinical trials** of autologous Vγ9Vδ2 cells and of in‑vivo activation (Wilhelm 2003 lymphoma/myeloma; Dieli 2007 prostate; Kobayashi, Nakajima, Bennouna, Abe — mostly modest, transient responses).
- **2012–2014 — BTN3A1 is the missing piece.** BTN3A1 (CD277) is identified as essential for phosphoantigen sensing; structural and functional work (Harly, Sandstrom, Vavassori, Sebestyen) establishes the "inside‑out" model in which intracellular phosphoantigen binds the BTN3A1 B30.2 domain.
- **2015 — the protumor side hardens.** γδ T17 biology is causally linked to metastasis (Coffelt et al., *Nature* 2015 — IL‑17⁺ γδ cells drive neutrophil‑mediated metastasis in breast cancer; Wu et al. and others in colorectal/HCC), forcing a more nuanced view than "γδ = good."
- **2016–2020 — Vδ1 / DOT cells and the adaptive‑like view.** Attention broadens beyond blood Vγ9Vδ2 to tissue‑resident **Vδ1** cells (clonally expanded, "adaptive‑like," CMV‑shaped) and to scalable **Delta One T (DOT)** cell expansion protocols, positioning Vδ1 as a solid‑tumor‑oriented platform.
- **2020 — BTN2A1 completes the recognition model.** BTN2A1 is shown to bind the Vγ9 TCR directly and to act together with BTN3A1 (Rigau, Karunakaran, Vavassori 2020), giving a coherent two‑butyrophilin model and a new agonist drug target.
- **2018–present — engineering era.** CAR‑γδ cells (e.g. GD2‑CAR Vγ9Vδ2, Capsomidis 2018), defined‑γδ‑TCR transfer into αβ cells (**TEGs**, Sebestyen/Kuball), **antibody‑TCR** fusions, and **bispecific γδ‑engagers** appear. Allogeneic off‑the‑shelf programs mature: **ADI‑001** (allogeneic anti‑CD20 CAR Vδ1; Neelapu/Adicet), **DOT cells**, and agonist antibodies (**ICT01**, anti‑BTN3A) enter clinical testing.
- **2022–2026 — manufacturing, combinations, and CNS/solid‑tumor push.** Non‑viral CAR‑γδ manufacturing, clinical‑grade automated platforms, intrathecal/intracranial allogeneic CAR‑γδ for CNS disease, oncolytic‑virus and checkpoint combinations, and single‑cell dissections of γδ TILs dominate the most recent literature.

## Conventions used in this synthesis

- **Subset nomenclature.** "Vγ9Vδ2" (also written Vγ2Vδ2) = the phosphoantigen‑reactive, blood‑dominant subset. "Vδ1", "Vδ3" = non‑Vδ2 subsets, mostly tissue‑resident. "γδ T17" = IL‑17‑producing γδ cells.
- **Citations.** Papers present in this collection are cited as `Author Year (PMIDxxxxxxxx)`; where the local PDF exists, see `index.tsv` for the path. Foundational papers outside the OA corpus are cited by author/year (and PMID where useful) without a local file.
- Capsule per‑paper extractions are in `10_per_paper_extractions.md`; they distill the design/finding, not the full methods.
