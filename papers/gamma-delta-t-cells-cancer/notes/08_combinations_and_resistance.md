# Combinations and mechanisms of resistance

Because monotherapy γδ products have been safe but only modestly effective in solid tumors, much of the recent literature is about **combinations** that overcome the tumor microenvironment, and about the **resistance mechanisms** those combinations must defeat.

## Combination strategies

### 1. ADCC: γδ cells + tumor‑targeting monoclonal antibodies
Expanded γδ cells upregulate **CD16**, so co‑administered approved mAbs redirect them by ADCC: anti‑CD20 (rituximab/obinutuzumab) in lymphoma, anti‑HER2 (trastuzumab), anti‑EGFR (cetuximab), anti‑GD2. This is the lowest‑barrier combination (reuses approved drugs) and a recurrent preclinical theme.

### 2. Aminobisphosphonate sensitization
Zoledronate raises tumor/accessory‑cell **IPP**, sensitizing targets to Vγ9Vδ2 killing and supporting in‑vivo expansion. Pulsed/pro‑drug formulations aim to localize the effect and limit exhaustion (PMID 28239463; bisphosphonate‑prodrug CAR‑γδ).

### 3. Checkpoint blockade
γδ cells in tumors express **PD‑1, TIM‑3, LAG‑3, TIGIT**; combining γδ therapy with **anti‑PD‑1/PD‑L1** relieves exhaustion. Examples: anti‑PD‑1 promotes tumor regression in a mesothelioma model (PMID 38077396); **anti‑PD‑1‑armored γδ T cells** in ovarian cancer (PMID, "Anti‑PD‑1 antibody armored γδ T cells enhance anti‑tumor efficacy in ovarian cancer"); ICT01 ± pembrolizumab (EVICTION). "Dual modulation of cytotoxic and checkpoint receptors" tunes DOT‑cell efficacy (PMID 40240620).

### 4. Radiation, oncolytic virus, and antigen release
Radiation upregulates stress/NKG2D ligands and death receptors (synergy), but can also expand protumor **γδ17** and cause **radioresistance** via IL‑17 (PMID 41055972) — a double‑edged combination. **Oncolytic virus + γδ‑TCR‑T** is explored for diffuse midline glioma (PMID 40076788). Localized ablative immunotherapy reshapes the γδ‑TIL transcriptome (PMID, "Localized ablative immunotherapy…").

### 5. Cytokine support
**IL‑15** (agonists, armoring) sustains γδ persistence/fitness better than IL‑2 with less Treg expansion (PMID 42039157 review); IL‑18/IL‑12 push IFN‑γ. Recombinant IL‑15 is in clinical PD studies.

### 6. Agonists + engagers
**Anti‑BTN3A agonist (ICT01)** and **γδ bispecific engagers** can be combined with checkpoint blockade or αβ‑directed bispecifics (e.g. mosunetuzumab co‑opting Vγ9Vδ2, PMID "Two Are Better than One").

## Mechanisms of tumor resistance / immune escape

1. **Loss/shedding of recognition ligands:** down‑regulation or proteolytic shedding of **NKG2D ligands (MICA/B, ULBPs)**; loss of CD155/CD112; reduced **butyrophilin (BTN2A1/BTN3A1)** surface display — directly blunting both TCR and NK‑receptor channels.
2. **Butyrophilin subversion:** tumor **BTN3A1** can drive Vγ9Vδ2 **exhaustion** instead of activation (cervical cancer); viral modulation of BTN2A1 (EBV BRRF1 in NPC, PMID 39769218).
3. **Metabolic hostility:** lactate/acidosis, hypoxia, adenosine, nutrient depletion impair γδ function (Vδ1's lactate resistance, PMID 42106736, is a partial countermeasure).
4. **Suppressive milieu:** TGF‑β, Tregs, MDSCs, and tumor‑derived IL‑1β/IL‑23/IL‑6 that **repolarize γδ toward γδ17**.
5. **Checkpoint upregulation / exhaustion** under chronic antigen.
6. **Poor trafficking/retention:** failure to home (chemokine‑axis dependence, e.g. CXCR3, PMID 42208977) means infused cells never reach the tumor.
7. **Antigen‑density limits** for CARs (addressed by high‑sensitivity γδ‑TCR/AbTCR designs, e.g. GPC2 at low density, PMID 41027430).

## Design implication
The winning combination logic is converging on: **(engineered, allogeneic, Vδ1‑or‑armored γδ product) + (TME‑reprogramming partner: checkpoint blockade, IL‑15 support, and/or antigen‑release modality)**, with explicit avoidance of conditions that expand protumor γδ17.
