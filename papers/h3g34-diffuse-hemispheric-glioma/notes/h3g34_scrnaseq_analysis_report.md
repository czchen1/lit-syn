# H3.3 G34R/V diffuse hemispheric glioma scRNA-seq analysis: literature synthesis

## Executive summary

H3.3 G34R/V diffuse hemispheric glioma (DHG-H3G34) should be analyzed as a distinct pediatric/AYA glioma lineage rather than as generic adult GBM. The strongest current model is that G34R/V tumors arise in fetal ventral-forebrain **GSX2/DLX+ interneuron progenitors**, then occupy a continuum of interneuron-progenitor, GABAergic/migratory neuronal, astroglial, cycling, hypoxic/stress, and immune-interacting states. Adult GBM AC/OPC/NPC/MES labels remain useful as secondary projections, but final annotations should prioritize G34-specific lineage programs and genetic clones.

## Key H3.3 G34R/V papers

### Foundational / classification

- Schwartzentruber et al., **Driver mutations in histone H3.3 and chromatin remodelling genes in paediatric glioblastoma**, *Nature* 2012. Discovery of H3F3A G34R/V and frequent ATRX/DAXX/TP53 co-alteration in pediatric GBM. URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/nature10833
- Wu et al., **Somatic histone H3 alterations in paediatric diffuse intrinsic pontine gliomas and non-brainstem glioblastomas**, *Nature Genetics* 2012. Reports G34R in non-brainstem pediatric GBM. URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3288377/
- Sturm et al., **Hotspot mutations in H3F3A and IDH1 define distinct epigenetic and biological subgroups of glioblastoma**, *Cancer Cell* 2012. Establishes H3F3A G34 as a distinct epigenetic/methylation subgroup. URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ccr.2012.08.024
- Korshunov et al., **Histologically distinct neuroepithelial tumors with histone 3 G34 mutation are molecularly similar and comprise a single nosologic entity**, *Acta Neuropathologica* 2016. Shows histologically heterogeneous G34 tumors share epigenetic identity; notes 3q/4q loss, PDGFRA/CCND2 differences, and MGMT prognostic association. URL: https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/46314/
- WHO CNS5 entity context: DHG, H3 G34-mutant is a pediatric-type diffuse high-grade glioma, CNS WHO grade 4; see reviews below.

### Lineage, single-cell, developmental programs

- Bjerke et al., **Histone H3.3 mutations drive pediatric glioblastoma through upregulation of MYCN**, *Cancer Discovery* 2013. G34 mutation alters H3K36me3 binding, induces developing forebrain/stem-cell programs, and upregulates MYCN. URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23539269/
- Chen et al., **Histone H3.3G34-mutant interneuron progenitors co-opt PDGFRA for gliomagenesis**, *Cell* 2020. G34R/V tumors arise from **GSX2/DLX+ fetal ventral forebrain interneuron progenitors**, lack oligodendroglial programs, show dual neuronal/astroglial identity, and often select PDGFRA-mutant clones at recurrence. URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7791404/
- Liu et al., **GABAergic neuronal lineage development determines clinically actionable targets in diffuse hemispheric glioma, H3G34-mutant**, *Cancer Cell* 2024. Defines a GABAergic interneuron-lineage hierarchy, developmental spatial niches, CRISPR dependencies, and CDK6 vulnerability; CDK4/6 inhibition shifts toward more mature interneuron-like states. URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11865364/
- EGA datasets: EGAS00001004301 for Chen et al. and EGAS50000000534 for 9 untreated DHG-H3G34 sc/snRNA-seq samples. URLs: https://ega-archive.org/studies/EGAS00001004301 and https://ega-archive.org/studies/EGAS50000000534

### Epigenetic / DNA repair / immune mechanisms

- Jain et al., **Histone H3G34R mutation causes replication stress, homologous recombination defects and genomic instability in S. pombe**, *eLife* 2017. Mechanistic evidence linking G34R to replication stress and HR defects. URL: https://elifesciences.org/articles/27406
- Fang et al., **Cancer-driving H3G34V/R/D mutations block H3K36 methylation and H3K36me3–MutSα interaction**, *PNAS* 2018. G34 mutations impair SETD2-mediated H3K36 methylation and mismatch-repair recruitment. URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6156674/
- Juretic et al., **Histone H3.3 G34 mutations promote aberrant PRC2 activity and drive tumor progression**, *PNAS* 2020. G34 mutations alter PRC2/H3K27me3 biology and oncogenic programs. URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7959516/
- Haase et al., **H3.3-G34 mutations impair DNA repair and promote cGAS/STING-mediated immune responses in pediatric high-grade glioma models**, *JCI* 2022. G34R downregulates DNA repair, increases DNA damage/DDR susceptibility, and activates cGAS/STING immune signaling. URL: https://www.jci.org/articles/view/154229
- Garcia-Fabiani et al., **H3.3-G34R mutation-mediated epigenetic reprogramming leads to enhanced efficacy of immune stimulatory gene therapy in diffuse hemispheric gliomas**, bioRxiv/PMC preprint. Preclinical immune-stimulatory gene therapy and JAK/STAT-relevant immune reprogramming. URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10312611/
- Kfoury et al., **Alternative lengthening of telomere-based immortalization renders H3G34R-mutant diffuse hemispheric glioma hypersensitive to PARP inhibitor combination regimens**, *Neuro-Oncology* 2025. ALT biology and PARP inhibitor combinations as therapeutic angle. URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11889718/

## Concrete scRNA-seq recommendations

1. **QC/integration**: perform sample-aware QC, doublet removal, ambient RNA correction, and conservative integration by patient/assay. Avoid overcorrection across primary/recurrent samples.
2. **Malignant calling first**: combine CNV inference (`SCEVAN`, `inferCNV`, `CopyKAT`) with expressed variants and matched DNA alterations (`H3F3A G34R/V`, `TP53`, `ATRX`, `PDGFRA`, `CDKN2A/B`).
3. **Separate malignant/TME clustering**: annotate non-malignant cells independently; recluster malignant cells after scoring/regressing cell cycle only when it obscures lineage programs.
4. **Metaprograms**: use cNMF/NMF/consensus factorization and retain programs reproducible across patients, not only clusters. Explicitly test G34 lineage programs, cell-cycle, hypoxia/stress, immune-response/interferon, and receptor signaling.
5. **Projection strategy**: project malignant cells to (a) DHG-H3G34 interneuron signatures, (b) fetal ventral forebrain/interneuron atlases, (c) pediatric HGG/histone-mutant references, and (d) Neftel adult GBM states. Report discordant/hybrid projections.
6. **Developmental trajectories**: use diffusion maps, Palantir, Slingshot, or Monocle3 with `GSX2/DLX/ASCL1` progenitor-like roots and `GAD1/GAD2/SLC32A1/DCX` endpoints. Use RNA velocity only with high-quality spliced/unspliced data and avoid overclaiming directionality from cross-sectional data.
7. **Niches/spatial**: if spatial/CODEX data exist, test interneuron progenitor stream/nest patterns, perivascular niches, hypoxia/necrosis, immune-myeloid neighborhoods, and neuronal/synaptic interfaces. Use ligand-receptor tools (`CellChat`, `CellPhoneDB`, `NicheNet`) only after robust cell-type annotation and spatial validation.
8. **Subclones**: infer CNV clones and map WGS/WES clones into scRNA. Test whether PDGFRA/EGFR/CCND2/MYCN/CDK6-amplified or CDKN2A/B-deleted clones map to specific states.
9. **Temporal evolution**: for newly diagnosed vs recurrent pairs, use mixed models and differential abundance (`miloR`, `scCODA`, `propeller`) with treatment, steroid exposure, necrosis, sample site, and chemistry as covariates. Distinguish clone expansion from state switching.
10. **Validation panel**: H3G34R/V, ATRX loss, p53, GSX2/DLX, GAD1/GAD2/VGAT, GFAP/AQP4, PDGFRA/pPDGFRA, CDK6, MKI67, myeloid/T-cell markers, and hypoxia markers.

## Caveats for newly diagnosed vs recurrent samples

- Most direct DHG-H3G34 scRNA evidence is from untreated/newly diagnosed tumors; recurrent disease is under-sampled.
- PDGFRA-mutant clones can be selected at recurrence; do not confuse genetic clone expansion with transcriptional plasticity.
- Adult recurrent GBM often shows mesenchymal/AP-1 shifts, but pediatric HGG longitudinal data indicate recurrence may involve neuromodulator/interferon changes and altered non-neoplastic compartments without a uniform adult-like MES transition.
- MGMT promoter methylation and TMZ response require cohort-specific interpretation; G34 tumors often have MGMT methylation, but treatment response is variable and confounded by age, extent of resection, radiation, recurrence timing, and sampling.
- snRNA vs scRNA, frozen vs fresh, necrotic content, steroids, and radiation/TMZ exposure can dominate state calls if not modeled.
