# Ionizable lipid design and structure–activity relationships

## The ionizable lipid as the primary determinant

The ionizable lipid is the functional core of the LNP — it complexes RNA at low pH during formulation, adopts a neutral charge at physiological pH for circulation, and re-protonates in endosomes to drive membrane disruption and cargo release. Its structure (headgroup, linker, hydrophobic tails) is the single largest determinant of both transfection efficiency and organ tropism.

## Headgroup chemistry

The amine headgroup's **apparent pKa** (typically measured by TNS fluorescence) controls:
1. RNA encapsulation efficiency (needs pKa > ~6.0)
2. Endosomal escape (optimal pKa 6.2–6.5 for hepatocytes; higher for extrahepatic)
3. Serum protein adsorption patterns (corona-mediated targeting)

Headgroup classes explored in this corpus:
- **Tertiary amines** (classical: DLin-MC3-DMA, ALC-0315-type): liver tropism
- **Piperazines / morpholines**: higher pKa, lung tropism (Dilliard 2023)
- **Imidazoles**: spleen targeting, pH-buffering (Han 2026; Zhang 2024 PMID 38770138)
- **Quaternary ammoniums**: permanent charge, strong lung targeting (SORT)
- **Dimethylamino / diethylamino**: tunable pKa by steric environment (Liang 2025)
- **Cyclic tertiary amines** (Ugi-derived): spleen-selective without additives

## Linker chemistry

The linker connecting headgroup to tails affects:
- **Biodegradability** (ester > amide > ether > alkyl)
- **Hydrolysis rate** → duration of expression
- **Isomerism** → altered pKa and membrane interactions (He 2026, PMID 42267985)

Linker types:
- **Ester linkers** (β-propionate, δ-valerolactone): most common, biodegradable, fast clearance (Heredero 2025)
- **Disulfide linkers**: glutathione-responsive intracellular release
- **Ketal/acetal linkers**: acid-cleavable, endosome-selective
- **Amide linkers**: more stable, longer expression window

## Tail architecture

- **Branched vs linear**: branched tails (e.g., phytyl, 2-hexyldecyl) increase cone-shaped geometry → promotes inverted hexagonal phase → better endosomal escape.
- **Tail length**: C10–C18 range; shorter tails favor extrahepatic delivery.
- **Unsaturation**: one or two double bonds in tails increases membrane fluidity and fusogenicity (linoleyl > oleyl > stearyl for endosomal escape).
- **Multi-tail (≥3)**: increasing from 2→4 tails changes the packing geometry and can shift organ tropism.

## Combinatorial libraries

The field has moved decisively toward combinatorial lipid libraries rather than rational single-lipid design:

- **One-pot multicomponent reactions**: Ugi (4-CR), Passerini (3-CR), Hantzsch, and aza-Michael additions enable rapid synthesis of 100–1000+ lipidoids from commercial building blocks.
- **Modular amine cores + tail aldehydes**: e.g., reductive amination of multi-amine cores with aldehyde tails (Naidu 2023, PMID 37092557).
- **Ring-opening of cyclic anhydrides/lactones** with amine nucleophiles.

Typical library sizes: 50–400 lipids per campaign, screened in vivo via barcoding (see notes/04).
