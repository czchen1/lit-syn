# Endosomal escape and intracellular delivery

## The endosomal escape bottleneck

LNPs enter cells via endocytosis, trapping cargo in endosomes. Only 1–4% of endocytosed nucleic acid escapes to the cytoplasm — the rest is degraded in lysosomes. Endosomal escape is the single biggest efficiency bottleneck in LNP-mediated delivery.

## Mechanisms of escape

### Inverted hexagonal phase (H_II) transition
The dominant model: as endosomal pH drops (7.4 → 5.5), ionizable lipids become protonated, interact with anionic endosomal membrane phospholipids, and form inverted hexagonal (H_II) structures that destabilize the membrane.

Key structural determinants:
- **Cone-shaped lipids** (large hydrophobic tails, small headgroup) favor H_II phase.
- **Branched tails** increase cone geometry.
- **Unsaturated tails** (linoleyl) promote membrane disorder.

### Proton sponge effect
Higher-buffering-capacity lipids (imidazole, polyamine headgroups) cause osmotic swelling of endosomes, contributing to rupture. This mechanism is more prominent in polymer-based systems but relevant for some lipidoids.

### Pore formation
Recent evidence (2024–2026) suggests that some ionizable lipids form transient pores in the endosomal membrane rather than full H_II transition — allowing mRNA to diffuse through.

## pKa as the central parameter

The apparent pKa of the ionizable lipid (measured by TNS assay, 6-p-toluidinylnaphthalene-2-sulfonate) is the most predictive single parameter:

| pKa range | Behavior |
|---|---|
| < 6.0 | Poor encapsulation, poor escape |
| 6.0–6.4 | Optimal for liver/hepatocyte delivery |
| 6.4–6.7 | Lung-selective window |
| 6.5–7.0 | Spleen/immune cell selective |
| > 7.0 | Permanent charge, toxicity concerns |

However, pKa alone is insufficient — lipids with identical pKa can have 100-fold different transfection efficiencies depending on tail geometry, linker chemistry, and formulation context.

## Organ-dependent escape

A critical insight from the extrahepatic targeting field: **endosomal escape efficiency is cell-type-dependent**. A lipid optimized for hepatocyte escape may perform poorly in:
- Lung endothelial cells (different endosomal pH dynamics).
- Dendritic cells (rapid phagolysosome acidification).
- Neurons (slower endosomal maturation).

This means organ-targeted LNP development requires re-optimization of endosomal escape in each target cell context.

## Enhancing escape

Several strategies explored in 2024–2026 papers:
- **pH-responsive polymer coating**: poly(propylacrylic acid) shell that disrupts membranes at endosomal pH.
- **Calcium phosphate co-precipitation**: CaPO₄ core dissolves at low pH, releasing CO₂ gas for osmotic rupture.
- **Photochemical internalization (PCI)**: light-activated endosomal disruption — limited by tissue depth.
- **Dual-lipid formulations**: mixing high-escape IL with high-encapsulation IL.
