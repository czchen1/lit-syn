# Diagnosis, imaging, ICP findings and differential diagnosis

## 1. Clinical phenotype

Symptoms are those of acute or subacute hydrocephalus and are indistinguishable
at the bedside from high-pressure disease:

- Depressed level of consciousness — the predominant sign in the pooled 195
  (Keough 2021, PMID 32736355); may progress to coma with brain-stem signs
  (Hunn 2014 mean GCS rise 4.6 on treatment, PMID 24275338).
- Headache, nausea/vomiting (nausea/vomiting in half of Smalley's children),
  lethargy, confusion.
- Cranial neuropathies (up-gaze palsy, Parinaud's syndrome with tectal
  compression), ataxia and gait failure, urinary retention/incontinence.
- Paediatric-specific: bradycardia, seizures, failure to thrive (Chen 2025, PMID
  40538927).
- Movement disorder: Holmes tremor with triventricular dilation (Chang 2022,
  PMID 35148507).
- **Postural pattern**: symptoms worse upright and relieved recumbent in the
  Foltz/Dias post-LP phenotype (Foltz 1988, PMID 3343612; Dias 1999, PMID
  10352418; Ozeki 2022 headache on head elevation, PMID 34526456) — the same
  orthostatic pattern as intracranial hypotension, which is a clue that the two
  entities share physiology.
- **Timing**: after a precipitant (LP, drain, leak) deterioration occurs within
  hours to days; after SAH the mean latency was 13.1 ± 7.7 days (Czorlich 2023,
  PMID 37004138); after the primary neurosurgical diagnosis it ranged from 7 days
  to 5 years (Akins 2011, PMID 21523524).
- **Early warning**: falling EVD output at unchanged drain height as the state
  develops (Akins 2011); quantitative EEG slowing many hours before clinical
  deterioration, reversing with CSF removal (Wang 2024, PMID 39740216).

## 2. Pressure measurement

- **Direct ventricular measurement is required.** Lumbar pressure may not reflect
  ventricular pressure when the compartments are disconnected (Chang 2026
  lumbar–ventricular discrepancy, PMID 42218510; Staykov 2011, PMID 21273925), and
  in post-LP cases the lumbar compartment *is* the sink. Shunt-tap manometry
  through the reservoir is acceptable in shunted patients (Hoffman 2026, PMID
  42224725), as is Ommaya puncture (Chen 2025).
- **Reported values**: 2.2–6.6 mm Hg (Pang 1994); mean 4.8 mm Hg (Lesniak 2002); −3
  mm Hg (Pandey 2017); −4 cm H₂O (Diaz-Romero Paz 2019); all < 70 mm H₂O and as
  low as −10 cm H₂O (Wu 2019); −5 to +10 mm Hg with excursions to −21 mm Hg
  (Salam 2025); ≤ 5 cm H₂O inclusion threshold in most series (Keough 2021;
  Datta & Tu 2025).
- **Continuous monitoring** (parenchymal or EVD transducer) is used to titrate
  therapy and to document the return of positive ICP as the end-point of
  sub-zero drainage (Pandey 2017; Godoy 2023 — drainage continued "with
  concomitant ICP monitoring at the neuro-ICU"). Background technique papers:
  Chari 2017 intraparenchymal monitoring for CSF disorders (PMID 28799016);
  Toma 2010 continuous ICP to investigate shunt function (PMID 20799857);
  Arroyo-Palacios 2016 ICP pulse morphology predicting EVD weaning (PMID
  27106888); Eide 2012 showing that ICP pulse-wave parameters barely change when
  mean ICP is negative until it falls below −15 mm Hg (PMID 22153319) — so pulse
  amplitude cannot be used to detect the state.
- **The trap**: a *normal* ICP (5–12 mm Hg) in a patient whose shunt is set to 10
  cm H₂O or whose EVD is at 15 cm H₂O is *inappropriately low* for the degree of
  ventriculomegaly and symptoms; the absolute number is less important than the
  mismatch between ventricles, symptoms and pressure (Hamilton & Price 2012;
  Gnanalingham 2004 "14 mmHg: a case of raised intracranial pressure?", PMID
  15201374 — retained as a reminder that thresholds are individual).

## 3. Imaging

- **Ventriculomegaly** out of proportion to the sulci: Evans index > 0.3 (Duan & Hu
  2024, PMID 38579413), rising frontal-horn ratio tracked as the treatment target
  (Houlden 2018, PMID 30009119), third-ventricular width used as the response
  metric (Bruk 2026, PMID 42732439: 13 → 8 mm and 13 → 4 mm).
- **Effaced convexity subarachnoid space and Sylvian cisterns** — the CSAS is
  collapsed because it is at lower pressure than the ventricles; the 2025 Chinese
  consensus makes this an explicit criterion, together with a rounded, enlarged
  fourth ventricle ("ball sign") implying fourth-ventricular outlet obstruction
  (PMID 40734409).
- **Transependymal / periventricular oedema despite low ICP** in 5/9 of Akins'
  patients — counter-intuitive and a pointer to the poroelastic model.
- **Enlarged perimedullary cisterns and brain-stem distortion** after LP-induced
  LPH (Vilela 2020, PMID 32712403), and descending transtentorial herniation
  with ventriculomegaly (Marianayagam 2017, PMID 28209310) — the caudal
  compartment is at *lower* pressure so structures are pulled down.
- **Absent cistern sign** and other LP-shunt function signs are unreliable
  (Kulkarni 1999; excluded).
- **Cine/phase-contrast MRI** documented CSF pathway obstruction in all 20 SILPAH
  patients (Hamilton & Price 2012) and reversed aqueductal flow toward the
  ventricles during LPH (Bateman 2026, PMID 42355921). Radionuclide or contrast
  ventriculography via the EVD has been used to localise the block (Rekate 2008;
  Filippidis 2011).
- **MR elastography** — very low shear stiffness during an episode, recovered two
  years later (Olivero 2016, PMID 27198914; Olivero 2021, PMID 33030603); Strand
  2018 (PMID 28962963) suggests MRE as the tool most likely to give an objective
  diagnostic marker.
- **Sunken flap / paradoxical herniation** in craniectomised patients (Salam 2025;
  Bateman 2026).

## 4. Proposed diagnostic criteria (converging sets)

| Criterion | Pang & Altschuler 1994 | Duan & Hu 2024 | Datta & Tu 2025 | Chinese consensus 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Symptoms of hydrocephalus / neurological worsening | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ (progressive deficits) |
| Ventriculomegaly | ✓ | Evans > 0.3 | change in ventricular size | progressive dilation; effaced cisterns; 4th-ventricle "ball sign" |
| Ventricular ICP | ≤ 5 cm H₂O | ≤ 5 cm H₂O | ≤ 5 cm H₂O | 0–70 mm H₂O on ventricular puncture |
| Response to drainage | improvement with sub-zero drainage | improvement with negative-pressure drainage | improvement after CSF drainage | (management criterion) |
| Exclusions | patent shunt | ventriculomegaly from neurodegeneration | — | high-risk history required |

Bakmeedeniya 2026 proposes a "diagnostic and management framework" but the
field still lacks a validated definition.

## 5. Differential diagnosis

| Entity | Shared features | Discriminators |
|---|---|---|
| **Shunt obstruction / high-pressure hydrocephalus** | identical symptoms, ventriculomegaly | ICP high on tap; shunt revision works. LPH: patent shunt at tap/revision, low ICP, revision fails (Filippidis; Hoffman) |
| **Shunt overdrainage / slit-ventricle syndrome** (Faulhauer 1978, PMID 742440; Serlo 1985/1989; Rekate 2008 shunt-related headaches, PMID 18259760; Pedersen 2023 definition review, PMID 36639536) | low or negative ICP, postural headache, subdural collections (McCullough 1974) | ventricles *small* or slit-like; Rekate type-1 SVS is the low-pressure form; treatment is *raising* resistance (anti-siphon, higher valve setting; Gruber 1984, PMID 6726390; Aschoff 1995, PMID 7621479) — the opposite of LPH. Bateman 2013 hypertensive SVS (PMID 23991845) shows SVS itself is heterogeneous |
| **Spontaneous / iatrogenic intracranial hypotension** (Mokri 2000, PMID 11113229; Binder 2002 intrathecal saline for obtundation, PMID 12188967; Samadani 2003 after lumbar drainage, PMID 12493112; Atkinson 1998 acquired Chiari, PMID 9452230) | postural headache, low ICP, sagging brain, obtundation, response to blood patch/Trendelenburg | ventricles small; no ventriculomegaly. LPH is best understood as intracranial hypotension *plus* a ventricle–SAS disconnection that inverts the gradient |
| **Post-dural-puncture headache in a shunted patient** (Koenig 2025, PMID 41357016) | post-LP, headache | check ventricular size — if enlarging, treat as LPH; blood patch carries extra considerations |
| **Normal-pressure hydrocephalus** (historical axis; Khan 2013 overdrainage in iNPH, PMID 23930853; Funnell 2020) | ventriculomegaly with normal opening pressure, gait/cognitive decline | chronic, elderly, B-waves and intermittent hypertension (Symon 1972); improves with *standard* shunting; "ultra-low-pressure NPH" is the overlap zone |
| **Cerebral venous overdrainage** (Barami 2016, PMID 27581321) | after CSF diversion, low ICP | may be a mechanism of LPH rather than a separate entity |
| **Sinking-flap syndrome / paradoxical herniation** | craniectomy, sub-atmospheric physiology, deterioration on LP or drainage | flap sunken, midline shift away from defect; cranioplasty is curative; LPH may coexist or follow (Bateman 2026; Bhatjiwale 2023) |
| **External hydrocephalus of infancy** (Sainz 2019, PMID 30474714) | infant macrocephaly | enlarged SAS with small ventricles from venous hypertension — the mirror image |
| **Ventriculomegaly ex vacuo / neurodegeneration** | large ventricles, normal ICP | no progression with symptoms; no response to drainage (Duan & Hu exclusion) |
| **Tension pneumocephalus with skull-base leak** (Stevens 2024, PMID 39220674) | post-shunt deterioration, leak | air on CT; both may coexist |

## 6. Practical diagnostic sequence distilled from the algorithms (Keough 2021; Casado Pellejero 2022; Hoffman 2026; Duan & Hu 2024)

1. Suspect LPH in any patient with symptomatic ventriculomegaly and (a) a patent
   shunt, (b) a recent LP/lumbar drain/CSF leak/craniectomy, (c) an EVD that
   drains little at standard height, or (d) SAH/IVH with clinical stagnation at
   ~2 weeks.
2. Measure ventricular pressure directly (EVD, shunt reservoir, Ommaya).
3. Confirm shunt patency (tap, shuntogram) rather than revising empirically.
4. Image for the sink and the block: CT for ventricles, sulci, flap, pneumocephalus;
   MRI with cine/phase-contrast for aqueduct/fourth-ventricle outlet patency; look
   for pseudomeningocele, skull-base defect, spinal CSF collection.
5. Therapeutic test: lower EVD to 0 then sub-zero targeting 5–15 mL/h; clinical
   and radiological improvement within 24–72 h confirms the diagnosis.
6. Exclude/treat coexisting infection, hyponatraemia and hypocapnia, which
   lower turgor.
