# 08 · How the sub-zero EVD is actually titrated — source by source

Scope: what each paper says about (a) the starting height and how far/how fast
it is lowered, (b) what is measured while drainage is sub-zero (ICP, output,
CT, examination), (c) how "response" and "stability" are judged and how often,
and (d) the rule for stopping the descent and starting the ascent. Quotes are
verbatim; FT = full text read (OA XML mirrored in `fulltext/`, or the
publisher PDF for Akins 2011 and Wu 2019); otherwise the abstract is the only
source and that is said. Where a paper is silent on an item it is listed as
"not stated" rather than inferred.

## A. Sources that describe a titration method

### Pang & Altschuler 1994 (PMID 7808607) — abstract only
- Setting / depth: "prolonged external ventricular drainage at subzero
  pressures (mean external ventricular drainage nadir pressure of −5.7 ± 3.6
  mm Hg, for a mean period of 22.2 days)". The reference point (external
  auditory canal) and the "subzero method" name come from Akins' and Wu's
  descriptions of this paper, not from the abstract.
- What was measured: ICP (2.2–6.6 mm Hg at peak symptoms), ventricular/
  biparietal ratio on CT, pressure–volume index in three patients.
- Response criterion: "During external ventricular drainage treatment,
  symptoms correlated only with ventricular size and not with ICP." Foster's
  restatement of Pang's method: "prolonged subatmospheric pressure drainage of
  the ventricular system until the ventricular shrinkage was maintained at a
  positive pressure, and a new shunt system could be inserted."
- Interval, step size, stop rule: not stated in the abstract.

### Bergsneider 1999 (PMID 10520938) — abstract only
- "a protocol of progressive ventricular hypotension induced by external
  cerebrospinal fluid drainage"; "External ventricular drainage pressures as
  low as −30 cm H2O were required to reduce ventricular size."
- Endpoint: symptoms "completely reversed once a normalization of ventricular
  size was achieved" — ventricular size, not a pressure, was the target.
- Step size and interval: not stated.

### Owler 2001 (PMID 11599454) — abstract only
- "All five patients were tested for possible low pressure hydrocephalus by a
  period of external ventricular drainage at heights of 0 to −5 cm H2O below
  the reference point (external auditory meatus—EAM)."
- Response: four of five improved clinically and radiologically; described as
  rapid. Interval, further lowering, stop rule: not stated.

### Lesniak 2002 (PMID 12617236) — abstract only
- "All underwent intracranial pressure (ICP) monitoring and subatmospheric
  cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) drainage with an intraventricular or lumbar
  catheter." Mean ICP 4.8 mm Hg (0–10). "All patients improved only in the
  setting of negative pressure CSF drainage." No heights, steps or intervals.

### Akins 2011 (PMID 21523524) — FT, publisher PDF
The only series that writes the method out as a protocol.
- Entry criteria establish that positive and zero heights have failed first:
  "(4) clinical and radiographic response to EVD drainage at +5 to +10 cm
  height; (5) delayed neurologic decline accompanied by recurrent hydrocephalus
  despite confirmation of a patent EVD and normal ICP; (6) failure to respond
  to lowering EVD (0 to +5 cm); and (7) clinical and radiographic response to
  sub-zero drainage."
- Depth: "sub-zero EVD drainage (typically, −3 to −10 cm)". Reference point
  (from their account of Pang): "setting the EVD height below the level of the
  external auditory canal". Case 6 stabilised "with EVD at −3 cm height"; case
  4 "was lowered to +5 and then 0 cm without clinical or radiographic
  improvement" before going sub-zero.
- What was watched: daily EVD output (the paper's central observation — "As
  patients entered the LPH state, the ICP remained in a normal range yet daily
  CSF output from the external ventricular drain was reduced"; "When LPH
  patients were drained at sub-zero levels, daily CSF output exceeded baseline
  values for several days and then receded to baseline"), ICP via the EVD, and
  CT ("Neuroimaging was systematically reviewed"; transependymal fluid in 5,
  which "slowly resolved with sub-zero treatment"; "restoration of the
  subarachnoid space").
- Response / stability: "clinical and radiographic". Ascent rule: "the EVD was
  raised in small increments (typically 3 cm steps) every 3–5 days. Prior to
  raising the EVD to a more positive height, clinical and radiographic
  stability was confirmed. If patients relapsed during EVD weaning, then the
  process was repeated. Shunt internalization was performed once clinical and
  radiographic stability was confirmed at neutral or positive EVD settings
  (typically 0 to +3 cm)."
- Stop-lowering rule: implicit — descend within −3 to −10 cm until output
  rises and the ventricles/exam improve; no explicit floor or interval between
  downward steps is stated. Three of nine "were unable to tolerate positive
  pressure EVD drainage" and went to ventriculopleural or valveless shunts.

### Hunn 2014 (PMID 24275338) — abstract only
- Target is flow, not height: "titrated external ventricular drainage (TEVD),
  titrated to produce between 5 and 15 mL per hour of CSF drainage, including
  drainage under subatmospheric pressure if this was required to maintain CSF
  flow."
- Response measured: GCS ("mean increase 4.6, p=0.003") and ICP ("mean increase
  8.5") — i.e. ICP rising back toward normal was itself treated as a marker of
  recovery. Ventricular size reduced in all eight.
- Reassessment interval, depth reached, stop rule: not stated.

### Foster 2016 (PMID 27069743) — FT
- Start: "The EVD was initiated at a subatmospheric pressure, placed at a
  height ranging from 5 to 10 cmH2O below midbrain" (note: midbrain, not EAM,
  as the reference). Lowest setting in the illustrated case "−12 cmH2O".
- Ascent and its gate: "incrementally raised 1–2 cmH2O every 3–5 days. Prior to
  elevating the drain, a computed tomography (CT) scan was obtained to
  document stable or smaller ventricular caliber. If the patient demonstrated
  neurologic decline, the drain was again lowered to a more negative pressure
  and the cycle reinitiated."
- Endpoint: "Patients underwent shunt placement once they demonstrated clinical
  and radiographic stability with an EVD leveled at or above midbrain."
  Illustrated case shunted "with the EVD at a pressure of +5 cmH2O".
- Output: "We, too, noted decreased output during genesis of LPH with a
  significant increase in output during sub-zero drainage." No numeric target.
- Descent interval and stop-lowering rule: not stated beyond the 5–10 cm
  starting range; the paper is about ETV, not the titration.

### Pandey 2017 (PMID 28017745) — abstract only
- Case treated "under continuous intracranial pressure monitoring" from −3 to
  +5 mm Hg with "daily cerebrospinal fluid drainage >200 mL" plus positioning
  (head down 10–15°), neck/chest/abdomen binding, hypotonic fluids (Na 130–135)
  and PaCO2 > 40. Height, steps, CT interval: not stated.

### Diaz-Romero Paz 2019 (PMID 30710718) — abstract only
- "the EVD level was adjusted to produce between 5 and 10 mL/hour of
  cerebrospinal fluid under a subatmospheric pressure set at −5 cm H2O, and
  gradually raised in increments of 1 cm every 3 days until a positive ICP
  occurred." Endpoint = return of positive ICP; response = "excellent clinical
  and radiographic outcomes". CT interval: not stated.

### Wu 2019 (PMID 30611954) — FT, publisher PDF
- No titration protocol. Management text: "Precise CSF external drainage even
  at a subzero level, maintaining proper systemic effective blood volume, and
  expanding cerebral blood vessels with nimodipine … Neck wrapping, bellyband,
  horizontal head position, and even the Trendelenburg position can be used to
  improve ventricular pressure." They cite "the subzero method … the titrated
  EVD method, and the active pumping negative-pressure shunt system" as others'
  innovations and conclude "there are still no standardized treatments".
- What they measured (diagnostic, not titration): "ventricular pressure was
  determined by the average height of the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) water
  column when patients were in a quiet supine position and the head was flat
  for at least 15 minutes" — lowest −10 cm H2O (four patients), −5 (two), 0
  (two), "according to the 2 external auditory canal lines".
- Stop / shunt rule: "VP shunt should be considered as soon as the ventricular
  pressure rises above 50–60 mm Hg" (sic; almost certainly mm H2O) and only
  with normal temperature and blood count, sterile CSF "in all ventricles and
  the arachnoid space", and no pneumocephalus.

### Godoy Hurtado 2023 (PMID 37373809) — FT
- Depth: "four were treated with EVD, draining at a height of between 5 and 15
  cm below the external auditory canal (EAC) for several days"; one case
  "responded to EVD draining at negative pressures which were reached
  progressively to 15 cm below de EAC, with daily debts around 300 mL of CSF"
  (the abstract's "zero to −15 mmHg" is the same range mislabelled in mm Hg).
- Measured: "Intermittent ICP measurements with closed drains ranged from −2
  mmHg to zero"; in two patients an intraparenchymal sensor ("Continuous
  recording of ICP with the intraparenchymal sensor during subzero drainage
  showed negative pressures, a flat recording with no pathological waves, and
  minimal amplitude between systolic and diastolic values"); serial imaging.
- Endpoint / ascent: "It took 25 days at intensive unit care to recover normal
  ventricular size associated with level of consciousness maintained. Then
  level of EVD was progressively raised until Monro level. On day 40 … new
  shunt". Relapse seven days after shunting → second catheter and "start again
  negative drainage" for 20 days. Overall: drain "until ventricular size
  normalized". Step size and CT interval: not stated.

### Kuppler 2025 (PMID 40030163) — FT
- Failure at ≥ 0 first: "it was not possible to drain a sufficient amount of
  CSF when the EVD was positioned at an atmospheric pressure level ≥ 0 cm H2O".
- Start and target: "subatmospheric CSF drainage was performed via the EVD,
  starting at a level of −10 cm below the external auditory canal (EAC) with a
  drainage rate of 5–15 ml/hr."
- How response was judged: "The efficacy of drainage was determined clinically
  by monitoring the patient's consciousness, speech production, and vegetative
  symptoms such as nausea and vomiting. In addition, it was ensured that an
  adequate amount of CSF was drained per day, and serial cranial CT scans were
  obtained to assess changes in ventricle size." "an average drainage of 160
  ml CSF per day was needed to maintain a stable ventricle size."
- Planned ascent: "raising the draining chamber by 2 cm at 3-day intervals
  when the clinical parameters were stable" — which failed over 180 days
  (chronic NegPH); EVDs exchanged every 4 weeks for infection prophylaxis, and
  the patient still developed S. epidermidis ventriculitis.

### Salam 2025 (PMID 40418891) — FT (refractory single case)
- Sequence of settings: "EVD was set to −8 cm H2O with a drainage goal of 10
  mL/hr" → "forced hourly drainage for a minimum goal of 10–20 mL/hr" → new EVD
  "set at −10 cm H2O with a goal drainage of 15 mL/hr" → "−5 cm H2O, with a
  minimum drainage goal of 10 mL/hr" → "as low as −30 cm H2O for forced
  ventricular reduction" → "gradually raised to −10 cm H2O, which was
  maintained for the following week" → later "weaned to 0 cm H2O" → "a drainage
  rate of 15–20 mL/hr was targeted by lowering the drain to whatever negative
  pressure achieved that goal … The drain's height was adjusted hourly to allow
  spontaneous CSF flow by either raising or lowering it by 2 cm H2O."
- Measured: ICP continuously via EVD (−21 to +10 mm Hg over the course),
  hourly output, CT after each change of strategy (figures 2–5 are all CTs).
- Their literature summary of the method: "initially serial 1-hour ICP checks
  are recommended following shunt externalization or placement of an EVD. Once
  low or negative ICP is confirmed, aggressive measures must be undertaken to
  reduce the ventricular size, including large-volume aspiration of 30–50 mL of
  CSF and subsequent drainage of 10–15 mL/hr, with a willingness to lower
  drainage settings to less than zero to meet this target."

### Lin 2025 (PMID 40826122) — FT, narrative review (Chinese practice)
- "Significant variation exists in the initial external ventricular drain
  height setting, ranging from −20 to −5 cmH2O, achieved by one or more
  large-volume ventricular cerebrospinal fluid aspirations (30–50 ml), followed
  by drainage of cerebrospinal fluid at a fixed volume of 10–15 ml per hour."
- Baseline imaging after response, then ascend: "After the patient is
  clinically stable and the ventricles have shrunk in size, a CT or MRI (e.g.,
  rapid sequence MRI) scan of the head is performed to document the baseline
  ventricular size. The cerebral compliance is adjusted by gradually increasing
  the drainage strength (high) (1–2 cm every 3–5 days), and a stepwise decision
  is made when the EVD drainage height reaches above the level of the external
  auditory canal (≥ 0 cmH2O, with some authors suggesting 4 cmH2O), and
  clinical improvement is stabilized."

## B. Single cases / smaller reports with a usable detail

- Doshi 2022 (PMID 36600762, FT): "lowering the EVD bag sequentially (in 5 cm
  gradient) below the reference level of tragus, until, there was a clinical
  and radiological improvement. Moreover, then, again gradually, raising it up
  (5.0 cm/day), till the negative or low-pressure state is reversed back. Once
  the ICP values reached up to the 9.0 mmH2O, with documented improvement in
  patients clinioradiological state" → shunt revised. (The 5 cm/day ascent is
  the fastest reported; a single case.)
- Galbarriatu 2013 (PMID 23904087, abstract): "maximum negative pressure of −8
  cm H2O".
- Weisbrod 2021 (PMID 34462697, FT, post-SAH): "Only once her EVD was
  progressively lowered to 0 cm H2O did her mental status improve and
  ventricular size begin to decrease on follow-up CT imaging"; weaned and
  removed by post-bleed day 15 without a shunt — a response at zero, not
  sub-zero.
- Wang 2024 (PMID 39740216, FT, post-SAH): levels in mm Hg (15 → 0 → 10 → 0 →
  5); each rise produced falling output (172 → 51 mL/day), rising delta power
  and a falling alpha/delta ratio on quantitative EEG before the examination
  changed, and each fall in level reversed it — the only report using qEEG as
  a continuous response marker. Manual drainage of ~30 mL when the level was
  dropped.
- Bruk 2026 (PMID 42732439, FT): "while draining, the patient was alert with
  Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) 15/15, whereas clamping produced drowsiness (GCS
  13/15) and recurrent ventriculomegaly"; ICP ~4 mm Hg throughout. States the
  general problem: "its protocol is not standardized, and drainage timelines
  are highly variable … ventricular re-enlargement and neurological decline on
  each attempt to raise the drain".
- Chen 2025 (PMID 40538927, FT, paediatric pooled): no heights; drainage 41.89
  days (2–365); institutional cases via Ommaya puncture, median 105 days, with
  CT before/after each stage; one death from infection attributed to the long
  drainage.
- Casado Pellejero 2022, Hamilton & Price 2012, Keough 2021 (abstracts):
  algorithms exist but the abstracts give no heights, steps or intervals;
  Hamilton/Price and Casado favour early ETV to shorten sub-zero drainage.

## C. Comparison across sources

| Item | What the sources say | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| Reference level | External auditory canal/meatus (Pang via Akins, Owler, Godoy, Kuppler, Lin, Wu's measurement); tragus (Doshi); **midbrain** (Foster). | Near-consensus on EAC; Foster is the outlier. |
| Prove failure at ≥ 0 first | Akins (+5…+10 then 0…+5), Kuppler (≥ 0 failed), Weisbrod (responded at 0), Salam (ICP < 5 with limited drainage). | Consistent: descent begins only after zero has failed. |
| Starting depth | 0 to −5 (Owler); −3 to −10 (Akins); 5–10 below midbrain (Foster); −5 (Diaz-Romero); −10 (Kuppler); −5 to −20 (Lin's summary); −8 (Salam first setting). | Cluster at **−5 to −10 cm H2O**; nobody starts below −10 except by Lin's report of practice variation. |
| Reported floor | −8 (Galbarriatu); −10 (Akins typical); −12 (Foster); −15 (Godoy); −5.7 ± 3.6 mm Hg ≈ −8 cm (Pang mean nadir); −30 (Bergsneider; Salam, refractory). | Most stop by −10 to −15; −30 appears only in Bergsneider's shunt-nonresponsive series and Salam's refractory case. |
| Descent step / interval | Doshi: 5 cm steps; Salam: ±2 cm hourly to keep flow; Godoy: "progressively"; Akins, Foster, Owler, Hunn, Lin: **not stated**. | No source gives a fixed timetable for going down; the two that give any rule are single cases. |
| Target while down | Flow 5–15 mL/h (Hunn; Kuppler; Diaz-Romero 5–10; Lin 10–15; Salam 10–20); daily volume > 200 (Pandey), ~160 (Kuppler), ~300 mL (Godoy); or simply "until output exceeds baseline" as an observed signature (Akins, Foster). | Consensus that **flow, not pressure, is titrated**; numeric target varies 5–20 mL/h. |
| ICP monitoring | Via the EVD column (Wu: supine, flat, 15 min; Akins; Salam hourly at the start); intraparenchymal sensor in a minority (Godoy, Pandey). Hunn and Diaz-Romero use **ICP turning positive** as the recovery marker. | Universal that ICP is recorded; only Godoy/Pandey used a separate parenchymal sensor. |
| Clinical marker | Level of consciousness/GCS in every source; Kuppler adds speech and nausea/vomiting; Bruk clamp-test (GCS 15 open vs 13 clamped); Wang qEEG delta power. | Consensus on consciousness; qEEG is a single report. |
| Imaging cadence | CT **before every upward step** (Foster; implied by Akins' "radiographic stability"); "serial" CT (Kuppler, Godoy, Bruk); CT after each strategy change (Salam); Lin: one CT/MRI to set the baseline once ventricles are small. No source states a fixed interval such as 24–72 h. | Agreement on CT gating each ascent step; no agreed interval during the descent. |
| Stop-lowering rule | Ventricular size normalises / exam recovers (Pang, Bergsneider, Godoy, Doshi); output rises above baseline (Akins); flow target met (Hunn, Kuppler, Salam). | Implicitly the same rule everywhere: stop going down when CSF flows and ventricles/exam respond; there is no pressure floor defined as "enough". |
| Ascent rule | Akins 3 cm every 3–5 d; Foster 1–2 cm every 3–5 d; Diaz-Romero 1 cm every 3 d; Kuppler 2 cm every 3 d; Lin 1–2 cm every 3–5 d; Doshi 5 cm/day. Gate = clinical + CT stability; relapse → lower again and restart (Akins, Foster, Godoy). | Strong agreement on **1–3 cm every 3–5 days**; Doshi is the outlier. |
| End of EVD phase | Stable at 0 to +3 cm (Akins), ≥ 0 or +4 cm (Lin), at/above midbrain (Foster, +5 in the case), positive ICP (Diaz-Romero, Hunn), "Monro level" (Godoy), ventricular pressure > 50–60 mm H2O plus sterile CSF/no pneumocephalus (Wu). | Agreement that a shunt is placed once ventricles stay small at a **neutral-to-slightly-positive height**; Wu adds infection preconditions. |
| Duration | Pang 22 d mean; Godoy 10–40 d (one case 25 d to normal size, shunt day 40); Foster 73 d mean without ETV (14–257); Chen 42 d (2–365); Kuppler 180 d unsuccessful. | Weeks to months; refractory cases defined by inability to climb. |

### Bottom line
1. Every series lowers the drain only after drainage at 0 to +10 cm has
   demonstrably failed, and most start between −5 and −10 cm H2O below the
   external auditory canal (Foster: below the midbrain).
2. Descent is governed by CSF flow, not by a schedule: the drain is dropped
   until it drains (5–15 mL/h or output above the patient's pre-LPH
   baseline) and the ventricles shrink; there is no published interval or step
   size for the descent apart from two single cases (5 cm steps; ±2 cm hourly).
3. What is measured is consistent — level of consciousness, ICP through the
   drain, hourly/daily output and CT ventricular size — but only Foster ties CT
   to a rule (a scan before every upward step), and no paper fixes a
   reassessment interval such as 24–72 h.
4. "Stop lowering" = flow restored + ventricles smaller + exam better; pressure
   itself is not the target and no author names a pressure floor. Settings
   below −15 cm H2O occur only in refractory cases (Bergsneider, Salam) and
   were not curative in Salam.
5. The ascent is the standardised part: 1–3 cm every 3–5 days, each step gated
   by clinical and CT stability, with relapse returning the drain to the last
   effective height; shunt once stable at 0 to +5 cm (or positive ICP).
