# Low-/negative-pressure hydrocephalus — overview and executive summary

## What the entity is

Low-pressure hydrocephalus (LPH), negative-pressure hydrocephalus (NegPH/NePH),
very-low-pressure hydrocephalus (VLPH), acute low-pressure hydrocephalus (ALPH) and
the "syndrome of inappropriately low-pressure acute hydrocephalus" (SILPAH) are
names for one clinical paradox: **progressive ventriculomegaly with the symptoms of
intracranial hypertension (depressed consciousness, headache, vomiting, cranial
neuropathies, gait failure, bradycardia) while measured intracranial pressure is
normal, low (conventionally ≤ 5 cm H₂O / ≤ 70 mm H₂O) or frankly sub-atmospheric**
(down to −10 to −21 mm Hg in reported cases). Shunts, when present, are patent;
conventional shunt revision or a standard EVD at 10–15 cm H₂O fails because the
ventricular pressure never reaches the valve opening pressure or the drain
outlet. Clinical status tracks ventricular size, not ICP (Pang & Altschuler 1994,
PMID 7808607; Daniel 2002, PMID 12027920; Godoy Hurtado 2023, PMID 37373809).

The modern entity was defined by Pang & Altschuler (Neurosurgery 1994), who
treated 12 shunted patients with ventriculomegaly and ICP 2.2–6.6 mm Hg by
prolonged "sub-zero" external drainage and framed the mechanism as a viscoelastic
alteration of brain parenchyma. Precursor descriptions exist from the 1970s–80s
in shunted patients (McCullough & Fox 1974, PMID 4813717; McCullough 1986, PMID
3491341; Foltz & Blanks 1988, PMID 3343612). Before 1994, "low-pressure
hydrocephalus" was used in Europe as a synonym for Adams–Hakim normal-pressure
hydrocephalus; that literature is retained here as a separate axis so it is not
conflated with the modern syndrome.

## Corpus at a glance

- 231 curated records (1969–2026) from a 1,190-record Europe PMC harvest; 32
  open-access full texts mirrored.
- **Core entity axis: 79 records**, 70 of which name the entity in the
  title/abstract; the remainder describe the phenomenon (symptomatic
  ventriculomegaly with patent shunt and low ICP) without the label.
- Evidence is almost entirely observational: 34 case reports, 24 case series /
  cohorts, 3 systematic reviews (Keough 2021 pooling 195 patients, PMID 32736355;
  Bakmeedeniya 2026 pooling 215 patients from 30 studies, PMID 41525875; Chang 2022
  on Holmes tremor, PMID 35148507), 1 consensus statement (Chinese Neuroscience
  Society 2025, PMID 40734409) and no prospective comparative trial.
- Supporting axes: 50 brain-biomechanics / compliance / elastography papers, 30
  drainage-and-valve technique papers (largely EVD-weaning and programmable-valve
  literature that frames titrated drainage), 14 etiological-setting papers, 30
  adjacent differentials (slit-ventricle syndrome, overdrainage, intracranial
  hypotension, venous overdrainage), 28 historical LPH-as-NPH papers.

## Ten take-home points

1. **Rare, under-recognised, and iatrogenically amplified.** Roughly 200–250
   published cases exist (Godoy Hurtado 2023; Keough 2021; Bakmeedeniya 2026).
   Single-centre incidence after aneurysmal SAH is ~3.7 % (Czorlich 2023, PMID
   37004138); in neurosurgical patients with severe disorders of consciousness
   and ventriculomegaly, 60 % had low- or negative-pressure physiology (Zheng
   2023, PMID 36994490). Almost every patient has had at least one prior
   intracranial procedure (mean 3.0 in Hunn 2014, PMID 24275338; ~3.5 operations
   per patient in Wu 2019, PMID 30611954).
2. **Precipitants cluster into three groups**: (a) a CSF sink outside the ventricles
   — lumbar puncture or lumbar drain, skull-base/cranial-wound CSF leak,
   pseudomeningocele, over-draining cyst or spinal shunt; (b) parenchymal
   "softening" — SAH/IVH, ventriculitis/meningitis, radiotherapy, trauma,
   diffuse injury, long-standing shunting; (c) loss of cranial containment —
   decompressive craniectomy / sinking-flap physiology.
3. **The mechanistic debate has four live models** — viscoelastic/hysteresis
   (Pang; Lesniak 2002, PMID 12617236), poroelastic "boggy brain"/Darcy flux
   (Akins 2011, PMID 21523524), transmantle gradient from ventricle–subarachnoid
   disconnection plus loss of brain turgor (Rekate 2008, PMID 18590387;
   Filippidis 2011, PMID 21800965; Diaz-Romero Paz 2019, PMID 30710718), and
   cerebral venous overdrainage/windkessel impedance (Barami 2016, PMID 27581321;
   Egnor 2023, PMID 37382303; Bateman 2026, PMID 42355921). They are not mutually
   exclusive and the leading reviews treat them as interacting (Bakmeedeniya
   2026; Li 2024, PMID 38688300). MR elastography has shown reversibly low brain
   stiffness during an episode (Olivero 2016, PMID 27198914; Olivero 2021, PMID
   33030603).
4. **Diagnosis rests on the paradox plus a therapeutic test**: symptomatic
   ventriculomegaly, ICP ≤ 5 cm H₂O on direct ventricular measurement, patent
   shunt / failed standard drainage, and improvement with drainage at zero or
   sub-atmospheric pressure (Pang–Altschuler criteria; Duan & Hu 2024, PMID
   38579413; Datta & Tu 2025, PMID 41191101). Imaging shows effaced convexity
   sulci and Sylvian cisterns with disproportionate ventriculomegaly, often
   transependymal oedema despite low ICP, and — in the 2025 Chinese consensus —
   a fourth-ventricular "ball sign".
5. **Temporising treatment is titrated (usually sub-zero) EVD**, set to a target
   hourly output (5–15 mL/h) rather than a pressure, lowered stepwise (0 → −5 →
   −10, occasionally −15 to −20 cm H₂O), and then raised in 1–2 cm increments
   every few days as ventricles shrink and ICP becomes positive (Owler 2001,
   PMID 11599454; Hunn 2014; Diaz-Romero Paz 2019; Godoy Hurtado 2023). Drainage
   durations of weeks to months are typical (mean 41.9 days, range 2–365, in the
   pooled paediatric series of Chen 2025, PMID 40538927).
6. **Adjuncts that raise ICP or close the sink shorten or avoid sub-zero EVD**:
   neck wrapping / soft cervical collar (Filippidis 2011; Pandey 2017, PMID
   28017745; Bruk 2026, PMID 42732439), Trendelenburg/recumbency (Foltz 1988;
   Dias 1999, PMID 10352418), epidural blood patch for post-LP or lumbar-drain
   cases (Dias 1999; Smalley 2017, PMID 28362957; Vilela 2020, PMID 32712403;
   Suzuki 2021, PMID 35854689; Hoffman 2026, PMID 42224725), repair of skull-base
   or wound CSF leaks (Filippidis 2011; Stevens 2024, PMID 39220674), removal of
   the offending spinal/cyst drain (Ozeki 2022, PMID 34526456; Diaz-Romero Paz
   2019), and — a single striking case — theophylline (Salam 2025, PMID 40418891).
7. **ETV is the main strategy for reducing shunt dependence**: a "significant
   decrease" in shunt-dependent patients with ETV in SILPAH (10 vs 10 patients;
   Hamilton & Price 2012, PMID 22116443); 50 % ETV-only success in the Casado Pellejero algorithm (2022, PMID
   34998487); ETV plus valveless catheter gave 81 % excellent outcomes in 16
   adults, 19 % later needing a valved shunt (Villalonga 2025, PMID 41165379);
   but Foster 2016 (PMID 27069743) found
   no significant shortening of EVD time and in Chen 2025's pooled children 91 %
   ultimately needed shunt repositioning/revision.
8. **Definitive drainage needs a very-low-resistance conduit**: valveless VP
   shunts, programmable valves at the lowest setting without anti-siphon
   devices, "therapeutic siphoning" with non-siphon-control or adjustable
   gravitational valves (Bergsneider 1999, PMID 10520938; Trinh 2013, PMID
   22770726; Funnell 2020, PMID 32989519), shunting into negative-pressure
   cavities (pleura, atrium), valves placed below the clavicle to exploit the
   hydrostatic column (Carrera 2025, PMID 39951714), wide-bore custom
   peritoneal catheters (Galbarriatu 2013, PMID 23904087), active-pumping
   shunts (Kalani 2013, PMID 23380444), improvised ultra-low valveless systems
   (Badran 2018, PMID 30287249) and intermittent reservoir pumping for chronic
   failure (Cheng 2017, PMID 28264586; Kuppler 2025, PMID 40030163).
9. **Outcome is bimodal and set by two things**: speed of recognition and the
   underlying disease. Pooled mortality is ~11 % (Keough 2021); 83 % of children
   but only 49 % of adults returned to their pre-episode baseline; 77.5 % of
   children recovered in Chen 2025; but Wu 2019's 39 secondary adult cases (18
   with CNS infection) had a mean GOS-E of 2.7 and 8 deaths. Post-LP paediatric
   cases are the most benign subgroup (Smalley 2017). Predictors of futility are
   unsealed CSF leak, irremediable ventriculitis and permanently altered
   compliance (Houlden 2018, PMID 30009119; Bakmeedeniya 2026).
10. **The evidence base is fragile**: heterogeneous definitions (ICP thresholds
    of 0, 5 and 7 cm H₂O; NegPH vs LPH; acute vs chronic), publication bias
    toward rescued cases, no registries, and mechanistic claims that rest on
    single MRE or phase-contrast measurements. Every major review from 2019
    onward calls for standardised definitions and a multicentre registry.
