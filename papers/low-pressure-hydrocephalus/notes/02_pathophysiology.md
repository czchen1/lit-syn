# Pathophysiology — competing and interacting models

The central puzzle: ventricles enlarge and compress brain even though the
pressure inside them is not high. Every model must therefore explain either (i)
a *gradient* across the brain mantle that exists despite a low absolute
ventricular pressure, or (ii) a change in the *material* of the brain such that a
normal or low pressure is enough to keep it distended, or both. The systematic
review of Bakmeedeniya 2026 (PMID 41525875) identified four commonly invoked
models — viscoelastic change, transmantle gradients, poroelastic/glymphatic
dysfunction, impaired CSF pulsatility — and treats them as interacting. The
clinician-oriented review of Li 2024 (PMID 38688300) covers the same four under
the labels transcortical pressure difference, excessive cerebral venous drainage,
brain viscoelastic change and the "porous elastic sponge".

## 1. Viscoelastic softening and hysteresis (Pang & Altschuler; Lesniak)

- **Claim.** Chronic or intermittent distension (e.g. repeated shunt failures with
  high pressure) permanently alters the stress–strain behaviour of periventricular
  white matter. Hooke's-law elasticity is replaced by viscous creep: the brain
  behaves like a material that has been stretched past its yield point and does
  not recoil when pressure normalises. A *lower*-than-normal ventricular pressure
  is therefore required to restore shape (Pang & Altschuler 1994, PMID 7808607).
- **Hysteresis.** Lesniak 2002 (PMID 12617236) modelled 10 adults (mean ICP
  4.8 mm Hg) as a pressure–volume loop with hysteresis: the volume reached at a
  given pressure depends on the path taken. Only negative-pressure drainage moved
  the system back onto the low-volume limb; ventricles then stayed small at
  normal pressure. Godoy Hurtado 2023 (PMID 37373809) uses the lung analogy
  (inflation and deflation limbs differ).
- **Evidence.** MR elastography in a 19-year-old with shunted aqueductal stenosis
  showed very low shear stiffness during an LPH episode (Olivero 2016, PMID
  27198914) that recovered to near-normal two years later after a
  *high*-pressure shunt failure (Olivero 2021, PMID 33030603) — the only direct
  human measurement of reversibly reduced stiffness in LPH. Houlden 2018 (PMID
  30009119) argued that recovery of GCS despite fluctuating frontal-horn ratio
  indicates "retained viscoelasticity" and predicts salvageability. Supporting
  biomechanics: brain elasticity changes with ventriculomegaly (Sklar 1980, PMID
  6893601), reassessment of brain elasticity in hydrocephalus models (Taylor &
  Miller 2004, PMID 15212932), MRE stiffness falls in early-onset hydrocephalus
  white matter (Wagshul 2021, PMID 33631603) and rises in NPH with partial
  normalisation after shunting (Streitberger 2011, PMID 20931563; Freimann 2012,
  PMID 21538046; Fattahi 2016, PMID 26542235).
- **Weakness.** Does not explain why the syndrome is so often triggered by an
  acute external CSF sink (LP, leak) in patients whose brains were not
  chronically distended, or why neck wrapping works within hours.

## 2. Poroelastic "boggy brain" — Darcy flux of interstitial fluid (Akins)

- **Claim.** The brain is a fluid-saturated porous solid. When interstitial
  fluid accumulates (transependymal oedema, inflammation, radiation injury), the
  parenchyma loses turgor and its ability to resist deformation; ventricles
  enlarge at low pressure. Sub-zero drainage produces a transient "CSF diuresis"
  (drain output exceeds baseline for days) as interstitial water is pulled back
  into the ventricles along a Darcy flux, after which the brain re-stiffens
  (Akins 2011, PMID 21523524: 9 patients, transependymal oedema in 5 despite low
  ICP, EVD output *fell* as LPH developed and *rose* above baseline during
  sub-zero drainage).
- **Model support.** Poroelastic finite-element models of hydrocephalus (Peña 1999,
  PMID 10414573; Dutta-Roy 2008, PMID 18534602; Chou 2016, PMID 26671218; Kim
  2016 on periventricular lucency, PMID 26274984) show that small sustained
  gradients can produce large ventricular strain when permeability and
  elasticity are altered; Solamen 2021 (PMID 32331966) measured poroelastic
  properties of NPH brains during lumbar drainage. Duy 2024 (PMID 38798141)
  reviews hydrocephalus as "biomechanical instability of the brain–CSF interface".
- **Clinical corollary.** Akins' group used the "sub-zero method" successfully in all
  9 patients and internalised with low-pressure hardware; the model predicts that
  fluid shifts (hyponatraemia, low osmolarity) worsen LPH — consistent with
  Houlden 2018's observation that avoiding fluid shifts helped, and with Pandey
  2017 (PMID 28017745) *deliberately* lowering plasma osmolarity to raise ICP.

## 3. Transmantle gradient from ventricle–subarachnoid disconnection and loss of turgor (Rekate/Filippidis)

- **Claim.** Ventricular size is set by the pressure difference between the
  ventricle and the cortical subarachnoid space (CSAS), not by absolute ICP. If
  the CSAS is (a) disconnected from the ventricles by obstruction at the
  aqueduct, fourth-ventricular outlets, basal cisterns or tentorial incisura, and
  (b) drained to a lower pressure than the ventricles by an external sink (lumbar
  puncture/drain, skull-base leak, cyst–pleural shunt), then the ventricle is at
  higher pressure than the SAS even when both are sub-atmospheric; CSF cannot
  return to the convexities and the ventricles enlarge (Rekate 2008, PMID
  18590387; Filippidis 2011, PMID 21800965; Vassilyadi 1995, PMID 7666227;
  Diaz-Romero Paz 2019, PMID 30710718; Suzuki 2021, PMID 35854689). Rekate
  replaces "compliance" with **brain turgor** — the capillary-bed-dependent
  firmness that normally keeps ventricles small — which is reduced by ischaemia,
  radiotherapy, injury and low venous pressure.
- **Predictions that have held.** (i) Neck wrapping / jugular compression raises
  venous and hence CSAS pressure and turgor and reverses ventriculomegaly within
  hours to days (Filippidis 2011; Pandey 2017; Bruk 2026, PMID 42732439);
  healthy-volunteer MRE confirms jugular compression stiffens brain in
  proportion to jugular venous flow (Hatt 2015, PMID 26045579). (ii) Closing the
  sink (blood patch, leak repair, removing a spinal drain) can abort the syndrome
  without sub-zero EVD (Dias 1999; Vilela 2020; Suzuki 2021; Ozeki 2022; Hoffman
  2026). (iii) ETV, which reconnects ventricle and SAS, reduces shunt dependence
  (Hamilton & Price 2012). (iv) Lumbar–ventricular pressure discrepancies are
  measurable in obstructed systems (Chang 2026, PMID 42218510; Staykov 2011,
  PMID 21273925).
- **Counter-evidence and nuance.** Direct measurements have failed to find a
  transmantle gradient in communicating or non-communicating hydrocephalus at
  steady state (Stephensen 2002, PMID 11904027; Shapiro 1987 cats, PMID
  3598675; Eide 2010 on pulsatile gradient, PMID 20130957), while Conner 1984
  (PMID 6737056) did find one in experimental NPH. Advocates reply that the
  relevant gradient is small, dynamic and only present while a sink is active,
  and that MRI-derived transmantle pressures (Sincomb 2021, PMID 34385144) are
  on the order of Pascals — enough over time in a soft brain.

## 4. Cerebral venous overdrainage, windkessel and pulsatility models

- **Venous overdrainage (Barami 2016, PMID 27581321).** CSF diversion at low
  pressure lowers the pressure surrounding cortical veins and bridging veins,
  collapsing them or increasing venous outflow, reducing cerebral blood volume
  and turgor; the brain "deflates" toward the ventricles. Vilela 2020 (PMID
  32712403) invoked this for post-LP LPH in an adult; Datta & Tu 2025 (PMID
  41191101) found prior jugular/sigmoid sinus thrombosis in a third of their
  paediatric LPH patients and modelled venous thrombosis as a predisposing
  factor; Sainz 2019 (PMID 30474714) showed the mirror image — venous
  hypertension increases turgor and *prevents* ventriculomegaly in infant
  "external hydrocephalus".
- **Windkessel / CSF path impedance (Egnor 2023, PMID 37382303; Egnor 2026, PMID
  41791099).** In a lumped electrical model the cranium is a band-stop filter that
  shunts arterial pulsatile energy through CSF to the veins. Egnor classifies
  hydrocephalus by CSF-path impedance: obstructive = high resistance; NPH = low
  inertance and high compliance; **low-pressure hydrocephalus = high resistance
  *and* high compliance**. Ventriculomegaly is an adaptive response that lowers
  impedance, which is why it can occur at any absolute pressure. Related
  pulse-wave-encephalopathy work (Bateman 2002, PMID 12221445; Bateman 2008,
  PMID 18379767) and pulsatility-driven glymphatic flow (Nozaleda 2025, PMID
  40258946) feed this view.
- **Autoregulatory reset and capillary CSF absorption (Bateman 2026, PMID
  42355921).** In a craniectomy patient who passed through sinking-flap syndrome
  and LPH, phase-contrast MRI showed bilaterally reduced CBF that rose by 26 % after
  cranioplasty and *reversed* aqueductal CSF flow toward the ventricles during the
  LPH phase. Lumped-parameter modelling favoured arteriolar constriction from an
  autoregulation reset over venous compression, and the authors hypothesise
  increased CSF absorption via the capillary bed at low ICP.
- **Unified mathematical model (Wang & Lu 2020 preprint).** A U-shaped ICP–volume
  curve that claims to explain slit ventricles, NPH/LPH and paradoxical herniation
  in one framework; untested.

## 5. Compliance, elastance and pressure–volume physiology (supporting axis)

The 50 biomechanics papers give the vocabulary and measurements the LPH
literature borrows:

- **Pressure–volume compensation** (Czosnyka 1996, PMID 8778261; Czosnyka 2001 age
  dependence, PMID 11235954; Kim 2009 compensatory reserve index, PMID 19240611;
  Gholampour 2022 new definition of compliance after shunting, PMID 35979170).
  LPH is a state of *high* compliance / low elastance: large volume change for
  little pressure change — the opposite of slit-ventricle syndrome.
- **Age and species dependence of viscoelasticity** (Shulyakov 2011, PMID 21316637;
  Shulyakov 2012 acute experimental hydrocephalus, PMID 22806081; Jugé 2016 rat
  obstructive hydrocephalus microstructure and stiffness, PMID 26848844;
  Pattison 2010 feline MRE, PMID 20655045; Dirrichs 2019 infant shear-wave
  elastography, PMID 31464808). Infant brains are uniquely compliant — Rekate
  2020 (PMID 32488353) argues this is why infant hydrocephalus, sinking
  fontanelles and post-LP LPH behave differently.
- **MR elastography in NPH and dementia** (Perry 2017, PMID 28063896; ElSheikh
  2017, PMID 28570101; Murphy 2020, PMID 32058331; Aunan-Diop 2022 scoping
  review, PMID 34687356; Coelho 2022 systematic review, PMID 35488708; Karki 2024
  outcome prediction, PMID 38272572; Hersh 2022 operative applications, PMID
  35933785). Stiffness *increases* periventricularly in NPH and falls after
  shunting — the opposite direction from the LPH case reports, suggesting LPH
  is not simply an extreme NPH.
- **Historical theory** — Raimondi's unifying definition (PMID 8194058),
  Orešković & Klarica's critique of the bulk-flow hypothesis (PMID 21641963;
  choroid plexus role, PMID 28456718), Oi's evolution theory of CSF dynamics
  (PMID 16685545), Li 2008 kaolin models of cortical SAS obstruction (PMID
  18433747), Fukuhara 2001 shunt removal and compliance (PMID 11302655).

## 6. Synthesis: a two-hit, two-compartment picture

The most parsimonious reading of the corpus is that LPH requires **a soft or
low-turgor brain** (hit 1: haemorrhage, infection, radiation, chronic
distension, craniectomy, venous thrombosis, immaturity) **and a pressure
differential that favours the ventricle** (hit 2: an external CSF sink or a
disconnected SAS at lower pressure, or shunt hardware that cannot drain below
its opening pressure). Treatments map onto the two hits:

| Intervention | Acts on |
|---|---|
| Sub-zero EVD | Makes the ventricle the lowest-pressure compartment; unloads interstitial water (hit 2, then 1) |
| Neck wrapping / collar / Trendelenburg / hypercapnia / low osmolarity | Raises venous pressure, CBV and turgor (hit 1) |
| Blood patch, leak repair, removing spinal/cyst drain | Removes the sink (hit 2) |
| ETV | Reconnects ventricle and SAS so the gradient cannot form (hit 2) |
| Valveless / siphoning shunts, negative-pressure cavities, active pumps | Permanent low-resistance path so the ventricle never sits above the SAS (hit 2) |
| Theophylline | Raises ICP in intracranial hypotension — mechanism uncertain, possibly adenosine antagonism and vasoconstriction restoring CSF volume/turgor (hit 1) |

The field has not tested these models against each other; the only human
material-property data are two MRE scans in one patient.
