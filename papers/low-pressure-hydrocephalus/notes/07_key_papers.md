# Key papers — per-paper extractions

Ordered chronologically within groups. "FT" marks papers whose open-access XML
is mirrored under `fulltext/`; the rest were read from Europe PMC abstracts.

## A. Foundational descriptions

**McCullough & Fox 1974** — *Negative intracranial pressure hydrocephalus in adults
with shunts and its relationship to the production of subdural hematoma* (J
Neurosurg; PMID 4813717). Shunted adults run sub-atmospheric ICP; subdural
haematoma is the consequence. Establishes that negative ICP is achievable and
harmful — the overdrainage pole of the same physiology.

**McCullough 1986** — *Symptomatic progressive ventriculomegaly in hydrocephalics
with patent shunts and antisiphon devices* (Neurosurgery; PMID 3491341). Patent
shunts with ASDs; ventricles enlarge symptomatically; removing the ASD or
lowering resistance helps. The paradox stated before it was named.

**Foltz & Blanks 1988** — *Symptomatic low intracranial pressure in shunted
hydrocephalus* (J Neurosurg; PMID 3343612). Upright, activity-related headache
and lethargy; low upright ICP; relief when recumbent/Trendelenburg. Motivated
the "zero-ICP" gravity-controlled shunt (Foltz 1994, PMID 8194062, 56 patients).

**Pang & Altschuler 1994** — *Low-pressure hydrocephalic state and viscoelastic
alterations in the brain* (Neurosurgery; PMID 7808607). 12 shunted patients,
ventriculomegaly, ICP 2.2–6.6 mm Hg, patent shunts; prolonged sub-zero EVD
restored ventricular size and symptoms; symptoms tracked ventricular size, not
ICP. Proposes viscoelastic change; defines the entity and its treatment.

**Vassilyadi, Farmer & Montes 1995** — *Negative-pressure hydrocephalus* (J
Neurosurg; PMID 7666227). Child with a spinal arachnoid cyst–pleural shunt
developing symptomatic ventriculomegaly at negative ICP; postulates a
transmantle gradient from the extra-ventricular sink. Coins "negative-pressure
hydrocephalus".

**Dias, Li, Freimark & Larson 1999** — *Low-pressure shunt 'malfunction' following
lumbar puncture in children with shunted obstructive hydrocephalus* (Pediatr
Neurosurg; PMID 10352418). Post-LP symptomatic ventriculomegaly with patent
shunts; recumbency ± epidural blood patch resolved it. Establishes the
paediatric post-LP phenotype and its benign treatment.

**Bergsneider et al. 1999** — *Beneficial effect of siphoning in treatment of adult
hydrocephalus* (Arch Neurol; PMID 10520938). Adults whose ventricles and
symptoms improved only when the shunt was allowed to siphon — the "therapeutic
siphoning" idea later applied to LPH.

## B. Series that defined management

**Owler et al. 2001** — *Low pressure hydrocephalus: issues of diagnosis and
treatment in five cases* (Br J Neurosurg; PMID 11599454). Five cases; stresses
direct ventricular pressure measurement and drainage at or below zero; first
UK series.

**Lesniak, Clatterbuck, Rigamonti & Williams 2002** — *Low pressure hydrocephalus
and ventriculomegaly: hysteresis, non-linear dynamics, and the benefits of CSF
diversion* (Br J Neurosurg; PMID 12617236). 10 adults, mean ICP 4.8 mm Hg;
ventricles normalised only after negative-pressure drainage and then stayed
small at normal pressure — a hysteresis loop.

**Daniel, Lee & Halcrow 2002** — *Low-pressure hydrocephalic state complicating
hemispherectomy* (Epilepsia; PMID 12027920). Post-hemispherectomy with CSF
rhinorrhoea; ventriculomegaly at low ICP; treated with sub-zero drainage then
low-pressure shunt.

**Clarke & Meyer 2006** — *Very low pressure hydrocephalus* (J Neurosurg; PMID
16961147) and **Houlden et al. 2018** — *Importance of frontal horn ratio and
optimal CSF drainage in the treatment of very low pressure hydrocephalus* (J
Neurol Surg A; PMID 30009119). VLPH after suprasellar/skull-base resection is
often refractory; Houlden's 4 cases suggest recovery of GCS despite persistent
ventriculomegaly identifies salvageable patients ("retained viscoelasticity")
and recommends frontal-horn-ratio tracking, volume optimisation and avoidance
of fluid shifts.

**Rekate, Nadkarni & Wallace 2008** — *The importance of the cortical subarachnoid
space in understanding hydrocephalus* (J Neurosurg Pediatr; PMID 18590387).
Reframes LPH as ventricle–CSAS disconnection with a lower-pressure CSAS and
loss of brain turgor; predicts benefit from ETV, neck wrapping, and closing
sinks. Companion: Rekate 2019 *Low or negative pressure hydrocephalus
demystified* (PMID 31103762) and 2021 commentary (PMID 34272587).

**Akins, Guppy, Axelrod, Chakrabarti, Silverthorn & Williams 2011** — *The genesis
of low pressure hydrocephalus* (Neurocrit Care; PMID 21523524). 9 neuro-ICU
patients; EVD output fell as LPH developed; transependymal oedema in 5 despite
low ICP; sub-zero method restored output above baseline ("CSF diuresis") and
resolved LPH in all; poroelastic/Darcy-flux model; all internalised with
low-pressure hardware.

**Filippidis, Kalani & Rekate 2011** — *Negative-pressure and low-pressure
hydrocephalus: the role of cerebrospinal fluid leaks resulting from surgical
approaches to the cranial base* (J Neurosurg; PMID 21800965). Three post
skull-base-surgery cases with leaks and ventricle–SAS block; treated with leak
repair, neck wrapping and drainage; formalises the leak-as-sink mechanism and
neck wrapping as therapy.

**Hamilton & Price 2012** — *Syndrome of inappropriately low-pressure acute
hydrocephalus (SILPAH)* (Acta Neurochir Suppl; PMID 22116443). 20 patients (10
without, 10 with ETV); all had EVD with drainage below zero; cine MRI showed
CSF pathway obstruction in all; ETV "reestablishes communication between the
SAS and ventricles" with rapid return of normal ICP dynamics and significantly
fewer shunt-dependent patients.

**Kalani, Turner & Nakaji 2013** — *Treatment of refractory low-pressure
hydrocephalus with an active pumping negative-pressure shunt system* (J Clin
Neurosci; PMID 23380444). Investigational active shunt for a patient failing
sub-zero EVD.

**Galbarriatu et al. 2013** — *Low-pressure hydrocephalus: a case report and
review* (Neurocirugia; PMID 23904087). Custom wide-bore peritoneal catheter
(thoracic-drain tubing) to minimise distal resistance.

**Trinh et al. 2013** — *Shunt overdrainage or low-pressure hydrocephalus?*
(Neurosurgery; PMID 22770726). Shunted NPH patient with LPH physiology treated
by converting to a siphon-permitting system — highlights the overdrainage/LPH
diagnostic fork.

**Hunn et al. 2014** — *Successful treatment of negative pressure hydrocephalus
using timely titrated external ventricular drainage and a low-pressure
ventriculoperitoneal shunt* (Clin Neurol Neurosurg; PMID 24275338). 8 adults,
mean 3.0 prior procedures; TEVD titrated to 5–15 mL/h including sub-atmospheric
drainage, then low-pressure VP shunt; mean GCS improvement 4.6; first series to
report acceptable long-term outcomes.

**Foster, Zlotnikov & Rekate (Foster 2016)** — *Analysis of low-pressure
hydrocephalus: does ETV shorten time to internalization?* (World Neurosurg; PMID
27069743). 16 patients; ETV did not significantly shorten EVD duration.

**Olivero et al. 2016 / 2021** — *Magnetic resonance elastography demonstrating low
brain stiffness in a patient with low-pressure hydrocephalus* (PMID 27198914) and
*Brain stiffness following recovery in a patient with an episode of low-pressure
hydrocephalus* (PMID 33030603). The only direct material-property data: very low
shear stiffness during the episode, recovered two years later.

**Barami, Sood 2016** — *The cerebral venous system and the postural regulation of
intracranial pressure: implications in the management of patients with
cerebrospinal fluid diversion* (Childs Nerv Syst; PMID 27581321). Venous
overdrainage model for low-pressure states after CSF diversion.

**Pandey, Ali, Vasenina & Prasad 2017** — *Management of negative pressure
hydrocephalus* (Neurol India; PMID 28017745). ICP −3 mm Hg reversed with
Trendelenburg, cervical wrapping, hypoventilation (raising PaCO₂) and lowered
plasma osmolarity — the most explicit "raise ICP medically" protocol.

**Smalley, Spiro & Dias 2017** — *Low-pressure hydrocephalus in children: a case
series and review of the literature* (Neurosurgery; PMID 28362957). 30 episodes
in 29 children; 37.9 % post-LP; post-LP cases: conservative 27 %, blood patch
18 %, drainage in the rest; median 4 vs 11 days of drainage for post-LP vs
spontaneous.

**Cheng et al. 2017** — *Clinical manifestations and treatment of low-pressure
hydrocephalus with ventriculoperitoneal shunt* (Chinese; PMID 28264586). 7
adults with shunts at lowest setting still enlarging; intermittent manual
compression of the reservoir several times a day improved 6/7 — the origin of
the "reservoir pumping" strategy later used by Kuppler 2025.

**Marianayagam et al. 2017** — *Trapped fourth ventricle and LPH* (PMID 28209310)
and **Badran et al. 2018** — improvised ultra-low-pressure valveless shunt after
SAH in haemangioblastomatosis (PMID 30287249): hardware improvisation case
reports.

**Strand, Balise, Leung & Boop 2018** — *Low-pressure hydrocephalus: a case report
and review of the literature* (World Neurosurg; PMID 28962963). 25-year search,
17 articles, 90 cases; argues MRE may become the objective diagnostic tool and
ETV a strong alternative/adjunct.

**Wu, Wang et al. 2019** — *Secondary low- or negative-pressure hydrocephalus:
clinical features, treatment and a new classification of hydrocephalus based on
pressure* (Chinese J Neurosurg / Neurosurg Rev; PMID 30611954). 39 adults, 18
with CNS infection, ~3.5 operations each, mean GOS-E 2.7, 8 deaths; proposes
high/normal/low/negative pressure classification (low < 70 mm H₂O, negative
< 0).

**Diaz-Romero Paz et al. 2019** — *Negative-pressure hydrocephalus: a therapeutic
challenge* (PMID 30710718). ICP −4 cm H₂O after external drainage of a cyst;
removal of the sink plus sub-zero EVD; transmantle-gradient discussion.

**Funnell et al. 2020** — *The ultra-low-pressure hydrocephalic state in NPH:
improvement after shunt siphoning* (Acta Neurochir; PMID 32989519). Shunted
iNPH patients who relapsed with large ventricles at very low ICP and improved
when the valve was reset to allow siphoning; defines the NPH overlap.

**Vilela et al. 2020** (FT) — *Low-pressure hydrocephalus after lumbar puncture in
an adult* (PMID 32712403). Enlarged perimedullary cisterns and brain-stem
distortion; epidural blood patch curative; venous overdrainage discussion.

**Keough, Isaacs, Urbaneja, Hamilton et al. 2021** — *Acute low-pressure
hydrocephalus: a case series and systematic review of 195 patients* (J Neurosurg;
PMID 32736355). 42 local + 153 literature; 53 children, 142 adults; decreased LOC
predominant; neoplasm and haemorrhage the commonest etiologies; 39 % of children
post-LP; shunt in situ 92 % children / 39 % adults; sub-atmospheric drainage the
main temporising step; shunt or ETV definitive; mortality 11 %; return to
baseline 83 % children / 49 % adults.

**Weisbrod et al. 2021** (FT) — *Angiogram-negative SAH and low-pressure
hydrocephalus after COVID-19* (PMID 34462697). Single case; adds a
systemic-inflammatory trigger.

**Casado Pellejero et al. 2022** — *Acute negative-pressure hydrocephalus:
management algorithm and value of early endoscopic third ventriculostomy* (World
Neurosurg; PMID 34998487). 5 patients; algorithm with early ETV applied in 4;
ETV alone succeeded in 2 (50 %), 2 needed shunts; 1 death from systemic
infection; 80 % good outcome.

**Czorlich et al. 2023** — *Acute low-pressure hydrocephalus in aneurysmal SAH*
(PMID 37004138). 15/406 (3.7 %); onset 13.1 ± 7.7 days; IVH presence/extent
associated; aLPH patients went on to chronic hydrocephalus and shunting far more
often than other SAH patients.

**Godoy Hurtado et al. 2023** (FT) — *Low- and negative-pressure hydrocephalus:
new report of six cases and literature review* (J Clin Med; PMID 37373809;
PMC10299038). Six cases (medulloblastoma ×2, TBI with bifrontal craniectomy,
craniopharyngioma, leptomeningeal glioneuronal tumour, shunted NPH); ~200 cases
in literature; symptoms follow ventricular size not pressure; sub-zero drainage
most used; neck wrapping, ETV, blood patch as alternatives; pathophysiology
"not clear" — permeability/viscoelasticity changes plus craniospinal SAS
imbalance.

**Zheng et al. 2023** — *Hydrocephalus in severe disorders of consciousness* (PMID
36994490). 68 DOC patients with ventriculomegaly; 60.3 % (41/68) had LPH/NegPH;
45.5 % had one-stage VP shunt + cranioplasty; individualised low valve pressure
by ventricular size and consciousness scores.

**Duan & Hu 2024** — *Low-pressure hydrocephalus: a narrative review* (PMID
38579413). Proposes criteria (Evans > 0.3, ICP ≤ 5 cm H₂O, response to
negative-pressure drainage, exclusion of neurodegeneration); risk factors
include prior radiotherapy, infection, haemorrhage, multiple surgeries; treatment
ladder EVD → ETV/valveless shunt.

**Li et al. 2024** — *Low/negative-pressure hydrocephalus: a review* (PMID
38688300). Four mechanisms (transcortical pressure difference, venous
overdrainage, viscoelastic change, porous elastic sponge); EVD/lumbar-cisternal
drainage first, ETV or shunt definitive.

**Wang et al. 2024** — *Quantitative EEG as early warning in post-SAH acute
low-pressure hydrocephalus* (PMID 39740216). qEEG slowing preceded clinical
deterioration and reversed with CSF removal.

**Kuppler et al. 2025** (FT) — *Chronic negative-pressure hydrocephalus after
failure of prolonged drainage* (PMID 40030163; PMC11877371). Refractory to
low-gradient shunt, neck wrapping, ETV and prolonged sub-atmospheric EVD;
scheduled intermittent active reservoir pressing enabled outpatient management;
defines cNegPH.

**Salam et al. 2025** (FT) — *Theophylline for refractory acute negative-pressure
hydrocephalus* (PMID 40418891; PMC12105587). TBI, hemicraniectomy, shunt
infection, IVH; ICP −5 to +10 mm Hg (nadir −21) despite ~4 months of sub-zero
EVD (to −30 cm H₂O), two ETVs and ventriculopleural/ventriculoatrial shunts;
two doses of theophylline 200 mg via PEG followed by spontaneous drainage,
slit-like ventricles on CT at 4 h and sustained improvement; status epilepticus
as complication. Single case.

**Chen, Li et al. 2025** (FT) — *Low- and negative-pressure hydrocephalus in
children: a systematic analysis of 44 literature cases and 4 institutional
cases* (PMID 40538927; PMC12176887). Craniotomy (mostly posterior-fossa tumour)
the leading antecedent; > 50 % prior surgery; fatigue, vomiting, headache,
bradycardia, seizures, failure to thrive; mean external drainage 41.9 days (2–365);
conservative measures (posture, reservoir pumping, valve adjustment) help a
minority; 90.9 % need shunt repositioning; 77.5 % recover to baseline; poor
outcomes track underlying disease.

**Datta & Tu 2025** — *Pediatric low-pressure hydrocephalus: a case series and
re-analysis of a rare disease* (PMID 41191101). 6 children, ICP ≤ 5 cm H₂O;
low-resistance VP shunts (3) or negative-pressure cavities (3); prior
jugular/sigmoid sinus thrombosis in 2/6; venous-thrombosis model.

**Villalonga et al. 2025** — *ETV plus valveless VP shunt hybrid for LPH* (PMID
41165379). 16 adults meeting Pang–Altschuler criteria; mean KPS gain 56; 81 %
excellent, 19 % temporary improvement; 19 % needed a valved shunt.

**Carrera et al. 2025** — caudal (infraclavicular) programmable valve placement to
exploit the hydrostatic column, 3 patients after prolonged sub-zero drainage (PMID 39951714).

**Chinese Neuroscience Society / Neurosurgical Branch 2025** — *Expert consensus
on diagnosis and treatment of low-pressure hydrocephalus* (PMID 40734409).
Definition (< 70 mm H₂O, progressive ventriculomegaly, worsening deficits),
imaging criteria (effaced Sylvian/convexity SAS, fourth-ventricle "ball sign"),
candidate mechanisms (fourth-ventricular outlet obstruction, compliance
change), management recommendations.

**Bakmeedeniya et al. 2026** — *Management of low/negative pressure hydrocephalus:
a systematic review of etiology, pathophysiology and management* (PMID
41525875). 30 studies, 215 patients (1980–2024); four interacting mechanistic
models; management centred on sub-atmospheric EVD, low-pressure/valveless
shunts, ETV; proposes a standardised diagnostic and management framework;
prognosis governed by etiology and timely recognition.

**Bateman & Bateman 2026** (FT) — *Pathophysiology of sinking flap syndrome
associated with low-pressure hydrocephalus* (PMID 42355921). Phase-contrast MRI:
bilaterally reduced CBF rising 26 % after cranioplasty; reversed aqueductal flow toward
ventricles during LPH; lumped-parameter model favours autoregulatory reset over
venous compression; hypothesises capillary CSF absorption at low ICP.

**Bruk et al. 2026** (FT) — *Neck wrapping for paediatric low-pressure
hydrocephalus* (PMID 42732439). Two children; third ventricle 13 → 8 mm and 13 →
4 mm; both internalised afterwards.

**Hoffman et al. 2026** (FT) — *Post-LP low-pressure hydrocephalus in a shunted
adult* (PMID 42224725). Diagnostic sequence: shunt-tap manometry, patency
confirmation, imaging for sink and block; treated with drainage and blood patch.

## C. Mechanistic and modelling anchors from the supporting axes

- Sklar 1980 brain elasticity changes with ventriculomegaly (PMID 6893601);
  Shapiro 1987 no transmantle gradient in cats (PMID 3598675); Conner 1984
  gradient in experimental NPH (PMID 6737056); Stephensen 2002 no gradient in
  communicating hydrocephalus (PMID 11904027); Eide & Saehle 2010 pulsatile
  gradient (PMID 20130957).
- Czosnyka 1996/2001 pressure–volume compensation (PMIDs 8778261, 11235954);
  Kim 2009 compensatory reserve (PMID 19240611); Gholampour 2022 compliance
  after shunting (PMID 35979170).
- Peña 1999, Dutta-Roy 2008, Chou 2016, Kim 2016 poroelastic FE models (PMIDs
  10414573, 18534602, 26671218, 26274984); Solamen 2021 poroelastic MRE in NPH
  (PMID 32331966); Duy 2024 brain–CSF interface instability (PMID 38798141).
- Bateman 2002/2008 pulse-wave encephalopathy (PMIDs 12221445, 18379767); Egnor
  2023/2026 windkessel impedance classification (PMIDs 37382303, 41791099);
  Nozaleda 2025 pulsation-driven glymphatic flow (PMID 40258946).
- Hatt 2015 jugular compression stiffens brain on MRE (PMID 26045579); Sainz 2019
  venous hypertension and turgor (PMID 30474714); Shulyakov 2011/2012 and Jugé
  2016 animal viscoelasticity (PMIDs 21316637, 22806081, 26848844); Wagshul 2021
  reduced white-matter stiffness in early hydrocephalus (PMID 33631603);
  Dirrichs 2019 infant shear-wave elastography (PMID 31464808); Rekate 2020
  infant compliance (PMID 32488353).
- Raimondi 1994 unifying theory (PMID 8194058); Oi 2011 multi-categorical
  classification (PMID 21928018); Orešković & Klarica 2011 bulk-flow critique
  (PMID 21641963); Bergsneider, Egnor et al. 2006 *What we don't (but should)
  know about hydrocephalus* (PMID 16572631).
