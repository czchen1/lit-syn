**Summary**: when recognised and treated with titrated sub-zero drainage, most
patients regain their pre-episode status; mortality of ~10 % and poor
functional outcome in about a quarter to a half of adults is attributable mainly
to the underlying disease (SAH, TBI, tumour, ventriculitis) and to delayed
recognition. Children and post-LP cases do best; infected and skull-base/VLPH
cases do worst. Permanent shunt dependence is the norm (≥ 70 % without ETV, 50–80
% overall).

## 2. Prognostic factors reported

Favourable: paediatric age; post-LP precipitant; short delay to sub-zero
drainage; identifiable and closable sink; retained viscoelasticity (GCS improves
before ventricles shrink; Houlden); clear distal obstruction amenable to ETV.

Unfavourable: CNS infection; unsealed CSF leak; suprasellar/skull-base surgery;
multiple prior operations; decompressive craniectomy with DOC; large IVH burden
(Czorlich); need for drainage at ≤ −10 cm H₂O; chronic course > 3 months
(Kuppler).

## 3. Complications specific to treatment

- Sub-zero EVD: ventriculitis with prolonged drainage; subdural haematoma or
  hygroma from overdrainage (McCullough & Fox 1974, PMID 4813717); intracranial
  hypotension symptoms; catheter blockage as ventricles collapse.
- Neck wrapping: jugular venous thrombosis risk, patient intolerance.
- Blood patch in shunted patients: theoretical risk of shunt tract/lumbar
  complications (Koenig 2025, PMID 41357016).
- Valveless / siphoning shunts: subdurals, slit ventricles — the pendulum swings
  to overdrainage (Faulhauer 1978, PMID 742440; Serlo 1985, PMID 4064018;
  Pedersen 2023, PMID 36639536); Doshi 2022 (PMID 36600762) illustrates
  oscillation between overdrainage and VLPH in one patient.
- Ventriculopleural shunts: pleural effusion; failure under positive-pressure
  ventilation.
- Theophylline: seizures (Salam 2025).

## 4. Evidence quality

- **Design**: 3 systematic reviews of case-level data, 1 consensus statement,
  ~24 retrospective series/cohorts (largest single-centre n = 42, Keough; largest
  pooled n = 215), 34 case reports, no prospective study, no trial, no registry.
- **Definitional heterogeneity**: ICP thresholds ≤ 0, ≤ 5 cm H₂O, < 7 cm H₂O; some
  series include "normal" ICP with failed standard drainage; acute and chronic
  cases pooled.
- **Selection and publication bias**: successful sub-zero drainage and successful
  ETV are over-reported; unrecognised cases (likely frequent in DOC and SAH
  populations — Zheng 2023; Czorlich 2023) are absent.
- **Mechanistic evidence**: mostly inference from treatment response; one patient
  with serial MRE; one with phase-contrast CBF/CSF flow; no direct transmantle
  gradient measurements during an episode; animal models (Shulyakov 2012, Jugé
  2016, Li 2008 kaolin) address hydrocephalus compliance generally, not LPH.
- **Outcome measures**: GCS, GOS-E, mRS, "return to baseline", frontal-horn
  ratio, third-ventricle width — inconsistent and rarely blinded.
- **Language/region**: a substantial body of Chinese literature (Wu 2019; Cheng
  2017; Li 2024; Chen 2025; Zheng 2023; Chinese consensus 2025; Lin 2025) uses
  the < 70 mm H₂O threshold and reports higher prevalence in DOC/post-infectious
  cohorts than Western series — possibly true case mix, possibly definitional.

## 5. Open questions and a research agenda

1. **Definition**: adopt a single operational definition (symptomatic progressive
   ventriculomegaly + directly measured ventricular pressure ≤ 5 cm H₂O or ≤ 70
   mm H₂O + failure of standard-pressure drainage), with acute/chronic and
   LPH/NegPH sub-labels — a Delphi process is the obvious next step after the
   2025 Chinese consensus.
2. **Epidemiology**: prospective screening of SAH, posterior-fossa tumour,
   ventriculitis and craniectomy/DOC cohorts with routine ventricular pressure
   measurement when ventricles enlarge; the 3.7 % and 60 % figures need
   replication.
3. **Mechanism**: serial MR elastography and phase-contrast MRI during and after
   episodes; simultaneous ventricular and lumbar/cortical pressure recordings to
   test the transmantle hypothesis directly; interstitial-water quantification
   (diffusion, T2 mapping) to test the poroelastic model; venous sinus imaging to
   test the venous overdrainage/thrombosis association (Datta & Tu 2025).
4. **Treatment protocol**: standardise sub-zero EVD titration (height steps,
   output targets, weaning rate) and compare early ETV vs prolonged EVD in a
   multicentre registry; define when neck wrapping or blood patch can replace
   drainage.
5. **Hardware**: evaluate caudal valve placement, valveless systems, pleural
   shunts and active-pumping devices head to head for internalisation failure
   rates and overdrainage complications.
6. **Pharmacology**: methylxanthines (theophylline, caffeine) and other agents
   used for intracranial hypotension deserve a formal case series with ICP
   monitoring.
7. **Biomarkers**: EVD output trajectory (Akins), quantitative EEG (Wang 2024),
   ICP pulse morphology (Arroyo-Palacios 2016; Eide 2012) as early-warning tools
   for the transition into the low-pressure state.
8. **Long-term outcome**: cognitive and functional follow-up of survivors,
   incidence of later overdrainage/SVS after low-resistance shunting, and
   whether brain stiffness recovers (Olivero 2021) enough to permit conversion to
   standard valves.
