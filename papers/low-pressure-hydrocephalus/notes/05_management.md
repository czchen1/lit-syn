# Management — acute algorithm, definitive diversion, refractory disease

No randomised or prospective comparative data exist. What follows is the
consensus pattern extracted from the series, algorithms and reviews (Pang 1994;
Owler 2001; Akins 2011; Filippidis 2011; Hamilton & Price 2012; Hunn 2014;
Foster 2016; Smalley 2017; Wu 2019; Keough 2021; Casado Pellejero 2022; Godoy
Hurtado 2023; Duan & Hu 2024; Li 2024; Chen 2025; Datta & Tu 2025; Bakmeedeniya
2026; Chinese consensus 2025), with dissent noted.

## 1. Acute phase — restore brain volume

### 1a. Remove or counter the sink first where possible
- **Post-LP / lumbar drain**: flat or Trendelenburg positioning; stop the drain;
  epidural blood patch if not resolving in 24–48 h (Dias 1999, PMID 10352418;
  Smalley 2017, PMID 28362957; Vilela 2020 adult, PMID 32712403; Suzuki 2021
  before EVD, PMID 35854689; Hoffman 2026, PMID 42224725; Koenig 2025 cautions
  on blood patch in shunted patients, PMID 41357016). In Smalley's 30 episodes
  post-LP cases needed a median 4 days of drainage vs 11 days for spontaneous
  cases.
- **CSF leak / pseudomeningocele**: direct repair, lumbar drain *avoidance*,
  fibrin sealant (Filippidis 2011, PMID 21800965; Stevens 2024, PMID 39220674;
  Kumar 2025 extra-axial ETV variant for persistent leak, PMID 41215506).
- **Cyst or spinal shunt**: ligate/remove (Vassilyadi 1995, PMID 7666227;
  Diaz-Romero Paz 2019, PMID 30710718; Ozeki 2022, PMID 34526456).
- **Sunken flap**: early cranioplasty (Bateman 2026, PMID 42355921; Zheng 2023
  combined cranioplasty + shunt, PMID 36994490).

### 1b. Raise venous pressure / brain turgor (non-invasive, hours)
- **Neck wrapping / soft cervical collar / bilateral jugular compression**:
  Filippidis 2011 (all 3 patients), Pandey 2017 (PMID 28017745 — combined with
  Trendelenburg, hypoventilation to raise PaCO₂ and *lowered* plasma osmolarity to
  reverse ICP from −3 mm Hg), Bruk 2026 (PMID 42732439 — two children, third
  ventricle 13 → 8 and 13 → 4 mm within days, both then internalised), Kuppler
  2025 (failed in chronic disease), Rekate 2019 review. Mechanism support: Hatt
  2015 MRE (PMID 26045579). Risks: jugular thrombosis, discomfort, poor tolerance
  in agitated patients; requires ICP monitoring.
- **Head-down positioning / recumbency**: Foltz 1988; Dias 1999; Pandey 2017.
- **Fluid and gas management**: avoid hypocapnia and hyperosmolar therapy; Houlden
  2018 (PMID 30009119) recommends avoiding fluid shifts; Pandey deliberately
  lowered osmolarity. This is expert opinion.

### 1c. Titrated external ventricular drainage (the reference standard)
Protocol synthesised from Pang 1994, Owler 2001 (PMID 11599454), Akins 2011
("sub-zero method"), Hunn 2014 (PMID 24275338), Foster 2016 (PMID 27069743),
Godoy Hurtado 2023 (PMID 37373809) and the paediatric reviews:

Each step is followed by the source wording it rests on (quotes from abstracts,
or from the full text where marked FT — Foster, Godoy, Salam and Chen from the
mirrored OA XML, Akins from the publisher PDF). Where the papers do not state a
number, that is said explicitly rather than filled in. Akins 2011 is the only
paper that lays out the sub-zero method as an explicit protocol; Pang 1994,
Owler 2001, Hunn 2014 and Wu 2019 are available here only as abstracts.

1. **Establish external ventricular access** — a new EVD or the existing shunt
   externalised; failure at conventional drainage pressure is part of the
   definition, not a step to be tried.
   - Pang 1994: "Seven patients failed to improve with multiple shunt revisions,
     including the use of low-pressure valves. In 11 patients, symptoms and
     ventriculomegaly were not reversed except with prolonged external
     ventricular drainage at subzero pressures".
   - Owler 2001: patients "failed to respond to apparently adequate CSF drainage
     via a functioning shunt (four cases) or external ventricular drain (one
     case)".
   - Foster 2016 (FT), Pang criteria: "(1) Neurologic decline at normal EVD or
     shunt pressures; (2) ventriculomegaly; (3) persistence of ventriculomegaly
     with ICP in the normal to low-normal range; and (4) clinical and radiographic
     response to sub-zero drainage".
   - Akins 2011 (FT) makes the failed conventional heights explicit in their
     inclusion criteria: "(4) clinical and radiographic response to EVD drainage
     at +5 to +10 cm height; (5) delayed neurologic decline accompanied by
     recurrent hydrocephalus despite confirmation of a patent EVD and normal ICP;
     (6) failure to respond to lowering EVD (0 to +5 cm); and (7) clinical and
     radiographic response to sub-zero drainage". Illustrated case 4: "EVD was
     lowered to +5 and then 0 cm without clinical or radiographic improvement."
   - Chen 2025 (FT), 28 paediatric cases with route recorded: "EVD, n (%) 12
     (42.86%) … VPS externalized, n (%) 16 (57.14%) … Ommaya, n (%) 4 (14.29%)".
   - Salam 2025 (FT): "subatmospheric CSF diversion via external ventricular
     drain (EVD) placement or shunt externalization with or without neck wrapping".
   - Catheter site and calibre are not specified in any series; individual
     reports use a frontal EVD (Salam 2025: "a new right frontal EVD").
2. **Lower the drain below the reference point until CSF flows** — start at 0 to
   −5 cm H₂O, go to −10 if needed; deeper settings are reported only in
   refractory cases. No paper specifies a fixed interval between downward steps.
   - Akins 2011 (FT), describing Pang's method: "sub-atmospheric ventricular
     drainage achieved by setting the EVD height below the level of the external
     auditory canal"; their own practice: "sub-zero EVD drainage (typically, −3
     to −10 cm)". Case 6 stabilised "with EVD at −3 cm height".
   - Owler 2001: "a period of external ventricular drainage at heights of 0 to −5
     cm H2O below the reference point (external auditory meatus—EAM)".
   - Foster 2016 (FT): "The EVD was initiated at a subatmospheric pressure, placed
     at a height ranging from 5 to 10 cmH2O below midbrain"; the lowest setting
     in their illustrative case was "−12 cmH2O".
   - Pang 1994: "mean external ventricular drainage nadir pressure of −5.7 ± 3.6
     mm Hg" (≈ −8 cm H₂O).
   - Wu 2019: "Ventricular pressure was lower than 70 mm H2O in all 39 patients,
     and the lowest value was −10 cm H2O" (this is the lowest *measured*
     pressure, not a drainage setting).
   - Godoy Hurtado 2023 (FT): drainage "at negative pressures which were reached
     progressively to 15 cm below the EAC".
   - Salam 2025 (FT), refractory case: EVD "set to −8 cm H2O", later "−10 cm H2O",
     and at the extreme "set as low as −30 cm H2O for forced ventricular
     reduction"; the drain "was adjusted hourly … by either raising or lowering it
     by 2 cm H2O" to allow spontaneous flow.
3. **Titrate to CSF output, not to a pressure** — 5–15 mL/h is the only explicit
   hourly target in a series; others give a daily volume.
   - Hunn 2014: "titrated to produce between 5 and 15 mL per hour of CSF
     drainage, including drainage under subatmospheric pressure if this was
     required to maintain CSF flow".
   - Salam 2025 (FT), summarising the literature: "large-volume aspiration of
     30–50 mL of CSF and subsequent drainage of 10–15 mL/hr, with a willingness to
     lower drainage settings to less than zero to meet this target"; their own
     goals were 10, 15 and 15–20 mL/h.
   - Daily-volume authors: Godoy Hurtado 2023 (FT) "daily debts around 300 mL of
     CSF"; Pandey 2017 (PMID 28017745) "daily cerebrospinal fluid drainage >200
     mL".
   - Akins 2011 describes the output *signature* rather than a target: "As
     patients entered the LPH state, the ICP remained in a normal range yet daily
     CSF output from the external ventricular drain was reduced."
4. **Expect a CSF "diuresis" when sub-zero drainage starts; monitor ICP if a
   parenchymal sensor is available.** The duration is given only as "several
   days"; Akins attribute the extra fluid to water leaving the parenchyma, not
   entering it.
   - Akins 2011: "When LPH patients were drained at sub-zero levels, daily CSF
     output exceeded baseline values for several days and then receded to
     baseline … When LPH was treated with the sub-zero method, a 'diuresis' of
     CSF ensued." (FT): "During sub-zero treatment, we observed a rise in EVD
     output which we attribute to reduction in ventricular volume and reduction
     in brain extracellular fluid"; sub-zero treatment "will 'wring out' the
     sponge-like cortical mantle … Water will flow from the parenchyma into the
     ventricular system."
   - Foster 2016 (FT): "We, too, noted decreased output during genesis of LPH
     with a significant increase in output during sub-zero drainage."
   - Godoy Hurtado 2023 (FT): "Continuous recording of ICP with the
     intraparenchymal sensor during subzero drainage showed negative pressures, a
     flat recording with no pathological waves, and minimal amplitude between
     systolic and diastolic values, suggesting a very distensible or 'compliant'
     brain."
5. **Response to sub-zero drainage confirms the diagnosis** (criterion 4 above).
   The series describe the response as rapid but do not fix a reassessment
   interval.
   - Owler 2001: "Four of the five patients showed rapid and significant clinical
     improvement … In the fifth patient there was no improvement with low
     pressure external drainage and no shunt revision was undertaken."
   - Hunn 2014: "TEVD resulted in improvement in Glasgow Coma Scale (mean
     increase 4.6, p=0.003), and increases in ICP (mean increase 8.5)"; "Mean
     delay from first negative ICP to TEVD was 1.8 days."
   - Pang 1994: "During external ventricular drainage treatment, symptoms
     correlated only with ventricular size and not with ICP."
6. **Wean upward slowly once ventricles are small and CSF drains at a positive
   pressure; expect weeks to months.** Both explicit protocols use a 3–5-day
   step interval.
   - Akins 2011 (FT): "the EVD was raised in small increments (typically 3 cm
     steps) every 3–5 days. Prior to raising the EVD to a more positive height,
     clinical and radiographic stability was confirmed. If patients relapsed
     during EVD weaning, then the process was repeated. Shunt internalization
     was performed once clinical and radiographic stability was confirmed at
     neutral or positive EVD settings (typically 0 to +3 cm)." "Three patients
     had refractory LPH and were unable to tolerate positive pressure EVD
     drainage" and went to ventriculopleural or valveless shunts.
   - Foster 2016 (FT): the EVD was "incrementally raised 1–2 cmH2O every 3–5
     days"; sub-zero drainage continued "until the ventricular shrinkage was
     maintained at a positive pressure, and a new shunt system could be
     inserted"; "patients required prolonged hospitalizations for protracted
     sub-zero EVD, often on the order of months". Mean time from first
     ventriculostomy to shunt was 73 days (range 14–257) without ETV and 114 days
     (0–236) with ETV, P = 0.16 — means, not medians — and the authors attribute
     the lack of difference to "an inherent bias to perform ETV on patients who
     were refractory to sub-zero drainage". One patient "underwent rapid EVD wean
     (in 8 days) and did not require shunting".
   - Pang 1994: sub-zero drainage "for a mean period of 22.2 days".
   - Chen 2025 (FT), pooled paediatric data: "the average number of days of
     external CSF drainage was 41.89 days, with a minimum of 2 days and a maximum
     of 365 days" (n = 28).
   - Wu 2019: "the time interval from onset to last VP shunt achieved was 31–3880
     days, with an average of 376 days" (29 survivors).
7. **Infection is both a precipitant and a complication of prolonged drainage,
   and predicts poor outcome.** The "vicious circle" wording in the earlier draft
   of this note is not in the sources; what they document is:
   - Wu 2019: "Eighteen cases had CNS infection" (of 39); Salam 2025 (FT) quoting
     the same series: "46.15% were complicated by intracranial infections, of
     which 87.5% were specifically in the ANPH subset compared to 35.48% of ALPH".
   - Akins 2011: "Conditions most commonly associated with LPH are: subarachnoid
     hemorrhage, chronic hydrocephalus, brain tumors, and chronic CNS
     infections." (FT): "We hypothesize that underlying inflammatory or
     degenerative changes alter periventricular brain permeability in LPH
     patients, for example, chronic CNS infection or chemical meningitis from
     subarachnoid hemorrhage." Conversely, they saw no infectious harm from the
     treatment itself: "No long-term complications were observed with low
     pressure shunting such as shunt infection or subdural hematoma."
   - Chen 2025 (FT), institutional cases drained via Ommaya for a median of 105
     days: this approach "increase[s] the risk of intracranial infection, and one
     patient ultimately died from intracranial infection".
   - Bakmeedeniya 2026: "delayed intervention, infection, and irreversible
     white-matter injury predicted poor recovery".
   - Salam 2025 (FT): the index case developed refractory NegPH after shunt
     infection ("removal of his shunt" under ceftriaxone/ampicillin).

Dissent: Hamilton & Price 2012 and Casado Pellejero 2022 (PMID 34998487)
prefer early ETV to avoid prolonged EVD; Chinese consensus and Li 2024 (PMID
38688300) recommend EVD/lumbar-cisternal drainage as first line with ETV or
shunt for definitive care.

### 1d. Pharmacology (experimental)
- **Theophylline** (Salam 2025, PMID 40418891): refractory acute NegPH after TBI,
  hemicraniectomy, shunt infection and IVH; ICP −5 to +10 mm Hg with excursions
  to −21 mm Hg despite sub-zero EVD (as low as −30 cm H₂O), two ETVs and
  ventriculopleural/ventriculoatrial shunts over ~4 months. After 48 h of oral
  caffeine without effect, "two doses of theophylline 200 mg twice a day" via PEG
  (borrowed from post-dural-puncture headache practice) were followed by
  spontaneous drainage at −18 cm H₂O and, on CT 4 h after the second dose,
  "complete collapse of the ventricles to an almost slit-like appearance";
  focal seizures progressing to status epilepticus began 4 h after the first
  dose. Single case; mechanism
  (adenosine antagonism → cerebral vasoconstriction → restored CSF production /
  turgor?) untested.
- **Acetazolamide / diuretics** are contraindicated in principle (they lower ICP
  further); mentioned only as things to stop.
- No other drug has been reported for the entity; the consensus and review
  literature is silent on pharmacology beyond avoiding ICP-lowering agents.

## 2. Reconnecting the compartments — ETV

- **Rationale**: if the syndrome depends on ventricle–SAS disconnection, a
  fenestration abolishes the gradient and may abolish shunt dependence (Rekate
  2008; Hamilton & Price 2012).
- **Data**:
  - Hamilton & Price 2012 (PMID 22116443): 20 SILPAH patients, 10 managed with and 10
    without ETV; ETV produced "a rapid return of normal ICP dynamics and a
    significant decrease in the number of shunt-dependent patients".
  - Casado Pellejero 2022 (PMID 34998487): algorithm with early ETV; 5 patients,
    ETV alone succeeded in 50 %, remainder needed a shunt; one death from systemic infection.
  - Villalonga 2025 (PMID 41165379): ETV in 16 adults, then valveless VP shunt if
    persistent; 81 % excellent outcome, mean KPS gain 56 points; 19 % system
    dysfunction requiring a valved shunt.
  - Foster 2016 (PMID 27069743): 16 patients, ETV did not significantly reduce
    the duration of EVD or shunt dependence.
  - Chen 2025 pooled paediatric: conservative measures and ETV rarely sufficed;
    91 % ultimately required shunt repositioning/revision.
  - Kuppler 2025: ETV failed in chronic NegPH.
  - Kumar 2025 (PMID 41215506): "extra-axial ETV" (fenestration through the
    cranial defect) for LPH with persistent leak.
- **Interpretation**: ETV appears to help a subset — adults with demonstrable
  distal obstruction and preserved SAS absorption — and is a reasonable step
  once ventricles have been re-inflated; it is not a substitute for sub-zero
  drainage in the acute phase and its comparative benefit is confounded by
  selection (Godoy 2023).

## 3. Definitive CSF diversion — minimise resistance, exploit gravity

The permanent shunt must be able to operate at the sub-zero pressures the brain
needs, i.e. the opposite design philosophy to anti-siphon devices:

| Strategy | Sources | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Programmable valve at lowest setting, **no** anti-siphon / gravitational unit | Pang 1994; Akins 2011; Marianayagam 2017; Chen 2025 (most children); Godoy 2023 | Standard first choice; risk of later overdrainage/SVS so needs follow-up and gradual up-titration |
| **Valveless** VP shunt | Villalonga 2025; Badran 2018 (PMID 30287249 — improvised ultra-low-pressure valveless system); Duan & Hu 2024 | Highest flow; watch for subdurals (McCullough 1974) |
| "Therapeutic siphoning" — non-siphon-control valve or adjustable gravitational valve set to permit upright siphoning | Bergsneider 1999 (PMID 10520938: adults improved with siphoning); Trinh 2013 (PMID 22770726: converting to siphon-permitting valve in shunted NPH with LPH physiology); Funnell 2020 (PMID 32989519: ultra-low-pressure state in NPH treated by siphoning) | Applies chiefly to adult NPH-overlap cases |
| Valve placed **below the clavicle** (caudal) to add a hydrostatic column | Carrera 2025 (PMID 39951714); Datta & Tu 2025 cite "shunt repositioning" | Converts body height into negative drainage pressure |
| Shunt into a **negative-pressure cavity** (pleura, atrium) | Salam 2025 (VPleural); Datta & Tu 2025; Chen 2025 (ventriculopleural in refractory children); Chiang 2001 (excluded — positive-pressure ventilation obstructs VPl shunts) | Pleural pressure −5 to −8 cm H₂O in spontaneous breathing; ineffective under mechanical ventilation |
| Wide-bore / low-resistance custom distal catheter | Galbarriatu 2013 (PMID 23904087 — thoracic-drain tubing as peritoneal catheter) | Reduces distal resistance |
| **Active-pumping / negative-pressure shunt** | Kalani 2013 (PMID 23380444 — investigational active shunt in a patient failing sub-zero EVD); background valve-technology literature: Aschoff 1995 (PMID 7621479), Czosnyka 2002 UK shunt evaluation (PMID 12111485), Chari 2014 (PMID 24405071) | Not commercially available; case-level evidence |
| Zero-ICP gravity shunt (ZIPS) | Foltz 1994 (PMID 8194062) | Historical; 56 patients, Foltz's own design |
| **Intermittent reservoir pumping** | Cheng 2017 (PMID 28264586: 7 adults with VP shunt at lowest setting; compressing the pump chamber several times daily improved ventricles in 6/7); Kuppler 2025 (PMID 40030163: scheduled active pressing as outpatient rescue) | Cheap, needs compliance and follow-up |
| Combined cranioplasty + shunt in craniectomised DOC patients | Zheng 2023 | Single-stage restoration of container and drainage |

Sequencing (Godoy 2023; Datta & Tu 2025; Chen 2025): internalise once the
patient tolerates EVD raised to ≥ 0–5 cm H₂O with stable ventricles; if
internalisation fails at the lowest valve setting, consider valveless/negative
cavity/caudal valve; if the patient re-develops LPH after a period of stability,
re-externalise and repeat sub-zero drainage — many patients cycle several times
(Wu 2019: 3.5 procedures per patient).

## 4. Chronic and refractory disease

Defined operationally as persistent or recurrent symptomatic ventriculomegaly
after adequate sub-zero drainage, ETV and low-resistance shunting (Kuppler
2025's *chronic negative-pressure hydrocephalus*; Clarke 2006 / Houlden 2018
*very-low-pressure hydrocephalus*; Lin 2025 *refractory hydrocephalus*).

- **Predictors of refractoriness**: unsealed CSF leak, ongoing ventriculitis,
  suprasellar/skull-base surgery, multiple prior operations, severe underlying
  brain injury, permanently altered compliance (Houlden 2018; Bakmeedeniya 2026;
  Wu 2019).
- **Options tried**: prolonged (months) sub-atmospheric drainage; ETV; low-gradient
  shunt; neck wrapping (failed in Kuppler's case); intermittent active shunt
  pumping (Kuppler, Cheng); ventriculopleural conversion; theophylline (Salam);
  MRE-guided timing (Olivero — stiffness recovery may indicate when a standard
  valve can be tolerated); cranioplasty; and, in the DOC population, acceptance
  of a permanent low-pressure system with continued rehabilitation (Zheng 2023).
- **Prognosis**: Houlden's 4 VLPH cases and Wu's infected subgroup had the worst
  outcomes (mean GOS-E 2.7); Kuppler's patient remained shunt-dependent but
  stable as an outpatient.

## 5. Paediatric specifics

- Post-LP cases: recumbency, then blood patch; many resolve without drainage
  (Dias; Smalley).
- Spontaneous cases after posterior-fossa tumour surgery: EVD titration exactly
  as in adults; long durations (up to a year); most end with a programmable valve
  at lowest setting, some with ventriculopleural or repositioned shunts (Chen
  2025).
- Neck wrapping is tolerated and effective in children (Bruk 2026).
- ETV alone is rarely sufficient (Chen 2025) — possibly because absorption at
  the convexity is also impaired by tumour/haemorrhage.
- Outcome: 77.5 % back to baseline; deaths driven by tumour progression.

## 6. What to avoid (from the failure reports)

- Standard-height EVD, standard valve settings or anti-siphon devices in a brain
  showing the paradox (McCullough 1986; Drake 1993; Trinh 2013).
- Empirical shunt revision without pressure measurement (Filippidis; Hoffman).
- Lumbar puncture or lumbar drainage in a patient with a patent shunt and
  obstructive pathology (Dias; Preuß; Ozeki) — or at least anticipate LPH.
- Osmotherapy, hyperventilation, diuretics in a patient with low ICP and large
  ventricles.
- Leaving a skull-base leak or pseudomeningocele untreated while draining
  sub-zero — the two sinks compete.
- Assuming that a "normal" ICP excludes hydrocephalus when the ventricles are
  enlarging and the patient is deteriorating.
