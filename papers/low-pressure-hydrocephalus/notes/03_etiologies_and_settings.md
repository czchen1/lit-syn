## 2. Precipitants by mechanism class

### A. An external or extra-ventricular CSF sink
- **Lumbar puncture** in a shunted child or adult with obstructive hydrocephalus
  (Dias 1999, PMID 10352418; Preuß 2013, PMID 23715811; Smalley 2017; Vilela
  2020, PMID 32712403; Koenig 2025, PMID 41357016; Hoffman 2026, PMID
  42224725). 37–39 % of paediatric cases are post-LP (Smalley; Keough). The
  shunt is patent; the LP creates a spinal SAS at lower pressure than the
  ventricle across an obstructed fourth ventricle/aqueduct.
- **Lumbar or spinal drainage** after SAH or posterior-fossa surgery (Ozeki 2022,
  PMID 34526456; Suzuki 2021, PMID 35854689; Dagnew 2002 acquired Chiari after
  lumbar drainage, PMID 12188966; Staykov 2011 lumboventricular gradient, PMID
  21273925; Bakhshi 2020 lumbar-drain complications, PMID 32711138).
- **Skull-base and cranial-wound CSF leaks / pseudomeningocele** (Filippidis 2011,
  PMID 21800965 — 3 cases after cranial-base approaches, all with documented or
  presumed ventricle–SAS block; Stevens 2024 skull-base leak with tension
  pneumocephalus, PMID 39220674; Smith 2016 posterior fossa pseudomeningocele,
  PMID 29506116; Udayakumaran 2018 leaks after tethered-cord surgery, PMID
  29605699; Bakhsh 2022 LP-shunt tract leak, PMID 35673636; Kumar 2025 extra-axial
  ETV for persistent leak, PMID 41215506).
- **Over-draining cyst or spinal shunts** (Vassilyadi 1995 spinal arachnoid
  cyst–pleural shunt, PMID 7666227; Diaz-Romero Paz 2019 external cyst drain, PMID
  30710718; Marianayagam 2017 trapped fourth ventricle treatment, PMID 28209310).
- **Hemispherectomy with CSF rhinorrhoea** (Daniel 2002, PMID 12027920).

### B. Parenchymal softening / loss of turgor
- **Aneurysmal SAH and IVH** — 3.7 % of SAH patients; IVH extent is the risk factor;
  aLPH patients later needed permanent shunts far more often (66.7 % vs 17.4 %)
  (Czorlich 2023). Case reports: Wang 2024 with quantitative EEG warning (PMID
  39740216); Weisbrod 2021 angiogram-negative SAH after COVID-19 (PMID 34462697);
  Badran 2018 haemangioblastomatosis SAH (PMID 30287249). Background: Chen 2017
  and Kuo 2021 reviews of post-SAH hydrocephalus (PMIDs 28373987, 34068783).
- **Ventriculitis / meningitis / chronic CNS infection** — 18/39 in Wu 2019; Akins
  2011; Salam 2025; Kuppler 2025. Infection is also the strongest predictor of
  poor outcome (Bakmeedeniya 2026; Houlden 2018).
- **Brain tumour and its treatment** — posterior-fossa tumours in children
  (medulloblastoma, ependymoma, astrocytoma), suprasellar lesions
  (craniopharyngioma; Houlden 2018's four VLPH cases followed suprasellar
  resection), tectal tumours (Chang 2022 Holmes tremor, PMID 35148507),
  leptomeningeal/intraventricular spread (Godoy 2023; Myers 2023), radiotherapy
  (Duan & Hu 2024 list prior cranial radiotherapy as a risk factor).
- **Traumatic brain injury** — Wu 2019; Svedung Wettervik 2022 (PMID 34414834:
  patients with "low-pressure hydrocephalus symptoms" had the best shunt
  response among post-traumatic hydrocephalus patients); Salam 2025; He 2026
  (PMID 42444806).
- **Long-standing shunt dependence** — 81 of the 231 records carry the
  `shunt_dependent_prior` tag; Pang's original series was entirely shunted
  patients; Keough found a shunt in situ in 92 % of children.
- **Sepsis / cardiovascular collapse** (McGovern 2013, PMID 23816322) and
  **COVID-19** (Weisbrod 2021) — single cases suggesting systemic
  inflammatory/vascular insults can precipitate the state.

### C. Loss of cranial containment
- **Decompressive craniectomy / sinking-flap physiology** — Godoy 2023 case 3;
  Salam 2025; Bateman 2026 (PMID 42355921); Bhatjiwale 2023 malignant oedema
  after cranioplasty (PMID 37680924); Ding 2014 review of craniectomy and
  hydrocephalus (PMID 25252237); Kutty 2018 (PMID 29725175) and Wan 2016 (PMID
  26971294) on managing ventriculomegaly and abnormal flap pressure; Joshua 2011
  papilloedema after craniectomy (PMID 21676532); Zheng 2023 combined
  cranioplasty + shunt in DOC patients. The open cranium abolishes the rigid
  container assumed by Monro–Kellie, so atmospheric pressure acts directly on
  the brain and small gradients translate into large volume shifts.

### D. Shunt hardware as precipitant
- Anti-siphon devices and gravitational units that prevent sub-zero drainage in a
  brain that needs it (McCullough 1986; Drake 1993; Trinh 2013; Funnell 2020).
- Programmable valves left at high settings after over-drainage complications
  (Marianayagam 2017; Doshi 2022 with raised intra-abdominal pressure, PMID
  36600762).
- Ventriculopleural/atrial shunts and the general risk of negative-pressure
  cavities — Chiang 2001 (excluded from core), Datta & Tu 2025 (used
  deliberately).

## 3. Paediatric versus adult spectrum

| Feature | Children | Adults |
|---|---|---|
| Dominant etiology | Posterior-fossa tumour (71 % neoplasm in Chen 2025), PHH of prematurity, congenital malformations | SAH/IVH, TBI, CNS infection, postoperative skull-base leak, shunted NPH |
| Precipitant | Lumbar puncture in 37–39 % | Usually spontaneous after surgery, drain or infection |
| Shunt in situ at onset | 92 % (Keough) | 39 % (Keough) |
| Distinctive symptoms | Bradycardia, seizures, failure to thrive, vomiting (Chen 2025; Smalley 2017) | Depressed consciousness, headache, gait failure, cranial nerve palsies |
| Course of post-LP cases | Often benign: recumbency ± blood patch; median 4 days of external drainage vs 11 for spontaneous (Smalley) | Blood patch effective in adults too (Vilela 2020) |
| ETV | Rarely sufficient alone; 91 % need shunt repositioning (Chen 2025) | Reduces shunt dependence (Hamilton); ETV-only success 50 % (Casado Pellejero) |
| Return to baseline | 83 % (Keough); 77.5 % (Chen) | 49 % (Keough); poor in infected/DOC cohorts (Wu 2019) |

Infant brain compliance (Rekate 2020, PMID 32488353; Welch 1980, PMID 7373397;
Dirrichs 2019) and the frequency of obstructive posterior-fossa pathology
explain why the paediatric phenotype is dominated by the LP-sink mechanism and
why it recovers more completely.

## 4. Frequency estimates

- Post-SAH: 15/406 = 3.7 % (Czorlich 2023).
- Severe DOC with hydrocephalus: 41/68 = 60 % low or negative pressure (Zheng
  2023) — the highest prevalence reported, suggesting that in this population LPH
  is the *rule* and that recovery of consciousness may depend on recognising it.
- Post-traumatic hydrocephalus: 14/29 shunted patients had "impeded recovery or
  low-pressure hydrocephalus symptoms" (Svedung Wettervik 2022).
- Titanium-mesh exposure after cranioplasty with a shunt: 9/10 had LPH (Zheng
  2025, PMID 39760505; excluded from core but illustrates the association with
  chronic intracranial hypotension).
- Cumulative published cases: ~90 (Strand 2018, 25-year search), ~195 (Keough
  2021), ~200 (Godoy 2023), 215 (Bakmeedeniya 2026). The Chinese consensus states
  incidence is "systematically underestimated" and no epidemiology exists.
