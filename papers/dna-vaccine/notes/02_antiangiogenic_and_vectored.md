# Anti-angiogenic DNA vaccines and live-vector delivery

Two related strands: (1) immunizing against a **tumor-vasculature self-antigen (VEGFR2/flk-1)** so the host attacks tumor endothelium, and (2) using **live attenuated bacteria** as in-vivo plasmid-delivery vehicles whose own PAMPs serve as adjuvant. Derouazi 2010 is OA; the Feng/flk-1 series is abstract-only.

## VEGFR2 / flk-1 anti-angiogenic DNA vaccines (Huazhong/Wuhan group)

A consistent program (2004–2005) immunized mice against the murine VEGF receptor-2 (flk-1), an antigen on proliferating tumor endothelium, using **orally administered attenuated *Salmonella typhimurium* SL7207** carrying a `pcDNA3.1-flk1`/`pcDNA3.1-VEGFR2` plasmid (the bacterium delivers the plasmid to gut-associated lymphoid tissue, priming systemic immunity).

- **Feng et al. 2004 (PMID 15587406, *J Huazhong Univ Sci Technol*; abstract only).** Oral SL7207/flk-1 inhibited intracranial **G261 glioblastoma** growth, reduced microvessel density, raised CTL activity, and prolonged survival.
- **Feng et al. 2005 (PMID 15890095, *Ai Zheng*; abstract only).** Showed a **specific anti-glioma angiogenesis immune response**: anti-VEGFR2 IgG by ELISA and CTL activity after gastric gavage with the VEGFR2 cDNA vaccine.
- **Feng et al. 2005 (PMID 15797625, *Cancer Letters*; abstract only).** Combined the **flk-1 DNA vaccine with an IL-12 plasmid**; the combination slowed tumor growth more than either alone, produced strong CTL against both flk-1 and tumor cells, and reduced mean microvessel density (antiangiogenic + antitumor).
- **Keke/Feng et al. 2004 (PMID 15650458, *Cancer Biother Radiopharm*; abstract only).** Oral SL7207/flk-1 vaccine + **intratumoral IL-12 plasmid** in **GL261** glioblastoma; combined antivasculature and antitumor effects with enhanced CTL.

**Take-away.** Targeting the (genetically stable, shared) tumor endothelium sidesteps tumor-antigen heterogeneity and immune escape. IL-12 co-delivery is the recurring amplifier. All work is murine and abstract-only here; no clinical translation of glioma flk-1 DNA vaccines followed in this corpus.

## Live bacterial vectored delivery

- **Derouazi et al. 2010 (PMID 21327126, *Bioengineered Bugs*, OA).** Built a live attenuated **_Pseudomonas aeruginosa_ type-III secretion system (TTSS)** vector that injects antigen directly into the host-cell cytosol from an **inducible, rapid-expression plasmid**. Using it as a screening platform against glioma, they compared TRP-2, gp100 and MUC18 antigens of varying length/epitope composition. Key finding: **similar CTL immunity and TCR-repertoire diversity** were seen across several TRP-2 constructs (TRP2₁₂₅–₂₄₃, TRP2L₁₂₅–₃₇₆, TRP2S₂₉₁–₃₇₆), **but only the longer TRP2L₁₂₅–₃₇₆ induced significant anti-tumor immunity** — i.e. CTL induction alone did not predict protection; **epitope length/composition and inclusion of strong CD4 Th epitopes** determined efficacy. The vector is promoted as versatile and fast for rapid antigen screening.
- The oral *Salmonella* flk-1 series above is the other major live-vector example; both exploit bacterial PAMPs as a built-in adjuvant and a non-injection (oral) or cytosolic delivery route.

## Why live vectors recur in this field

- **Built-in adjuvanticity**: bacterial LPS/flagellin and the alphavirus replicon (Yamanaka 2005, see `01`) supply innate signals that naked plasmid DNA lacks.
- **Cytosolic antigen delivery** (TTSS) favors MHC-I presentation and CTL priming.
- **Practical screening**: inducible bacterial expression plasmids allow rapid swap-in of candidate antigens/epitopes, which Derouazi exploited to learn that epitope composition — not merely CTL magnitude — drives protection.
