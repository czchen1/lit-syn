# Radiosensitization — pemetrexed + radiotherapy

Pemetrexed is an **intrinsic radiosensitizer**: by depleting nucleotide pools it both blocks DNA synthesis and starves the repair of radiation-induced DNA damage, and it redistributes cells toward the radiosensitive G1/S border. This makes radiation one of the best-documented modality synergies for pemetrexed, and the data are strikingly **schedule-dependent**.

## Schedule is decisive
- **Pemetrexed pretreatment before irradiation is superior to concurrent dosing** — prior nucleotide-pool depletion sensitizes NSCLC cells to subsequent radiation, eliciting a differential DNA-damage response (Dorn et al. 2016, PMID 27594806). Clinically, pemetrexed + radiation is usually given concurrently, so this identifies a potential optimization.
- **Cell-cycle redistribution** — pemetrexed accumulates cells near the radiosensitive G1/S border, with measurable dose-enhancement factors (Bhattacharyya et al. 2016, PMID 27838149).

## Enhancing the radiosensitization
- **Base-excision-repair inhibition (methoxyamine) enhances pemetrexed-mediated radiosensitization** of NSCLC cells and xenografts (PMID 27838149) — combining an antimetabolite radiosensitizer with a repair inhibitor.
- **One-carbon metabolism (MTHFD2) blockade** enhances chemo-radiation efficacy (PMID 33262939), linking folate metabolism to radioresponse.
- CHK1 inhibition sensitizes to both pemetrexed and radiotherapy in p53-mutant NSCLC (PMID 24418519; see DDR note), pointing to triple chemo-radio-checkpoint strategies.

## Practical implications
1. Pemetrexed is a **legitimate radiosensitizer**, most relevant in locally advanced NSCLC chemoradiation.
2. **Sequence matters**: preclinically, pemetrexed-first (nucleotide depletion) outperforms concurrent dosing — a testable clinical optimization.
3. Layering **DNA-repair/checkpoint inhibition** (BER, CHK1) or **one-carbon blockade** onto pemetrexed-radiotherapy is a rational way to deepen radiosensitization.
