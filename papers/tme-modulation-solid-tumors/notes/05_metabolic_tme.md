# Metabolic TME: adenosine, tryptophan, lactate and lipids

## Mechanism

Tumour metabolism suppresses immunity through soluble mediators and through nutrient
competition. Four sub-axes appear: the CD39/CD73/A2AR adenosine pathway
(PMID 20080644, PMID 36859386); tryptophan catabolism via IDO/TDO (PMID 39472902);
lactate and, mechanistically newer, protein/histone *lactylation*
(PMID 39934144, PMID 39883522, PMID 39561764); and lipid/cholesterol handling, where
cholesterol modulation potentiates CD8 responses (PMID 26982734) and 25-hydroxycholesterol
educates suppressive myeloid cells (PMID 38640930).

## Human evidence

- Adenosine: A2AR blockade produced objective responses in treatment-refractory RCC —
  the clearest clinical signal that a metabolic checkpoint can reverse resistance
  (PMID 31732494).
- Glycolysis: increased tumour glycolysis characterises resistance to adoptive T-cell
  therapy in human melanoma/NSCLC samples (PMID 29628419), making metabolic state a
  candidate selection biomarker for cell therapy.
- Lactylation is emerging as the mechanistic bridge from metabolism to epigenetic immune
  escape: H3K18 lactylation potentiates immune escape in NSCLC (PMID 39137401, indexed
  under `clinical_resistance`) and PD-L1 lactylation limits the benefit of a
  serine/glycine-free diet in colorectal cancer (PMID 39577415).

## What failed and why

ECHO-301 — epacadostat plus pembrolizumab in melanoma — is the definitive negative
phase 3 in this axis (PMID 31221619). Target engagement was likely incomplete and,
more importantly, kynurenine can be produced by multiple enzymes and cell types, so
IDO1-selective inhibition does not remove the metabolite. The general lesson the corpus
supports: metabolite-level readouts, not enzyme occupancy, should gate these trials.

## Open question

Whether dietary/systemic metabolic interventions can be dosed to favour T cells rather
than tumour cells; PMID 39577415 shows the same intervention doing both.
