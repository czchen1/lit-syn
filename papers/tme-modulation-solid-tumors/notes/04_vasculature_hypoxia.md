# Vasculature, hypoxia and vascular normalisation

## Mechanism

Abnormal tumour vasculature is both a barrier to T-cell entry and the source of the
hypoxic, acidic milieu that suppresses those T cells that arrive. The corpus supports
direct immunological effects of the angiogenic axis rather than purely perfusion effects:
VEGF-A upregulates inhibitory checkpoints on intratumoral CD8 T cells (PMID 25601652),
and HIF-1α directly transactivates PD-L1 with MDSC-mediated suppression under hypoxia
(PMID 24778419). Hypoxia also confers cytoprotection through the UPR/autophagy axis
(PMID 20038797).

## Human evidence

- The mechanistic rationale for combining anti-angiogenics with checkpoint blockade is
  high-endothelial-venule formation and lymphocyte entry (PMID 28404866), reviewed as a
  general strategy in PMID 29508855.
- HCC is where the combination became standard of care; the corpus captures this through
  the immunotherapy landscape review (PMID 34764464) and a critical appraisal of
  guideline-level recommendations (PMID 37535375).
- MSS/pMMR colorectal cancer: randomised phase 2 of anti-PD-1 + HDAC inhibitor + anti-VEGF
  is a direct test of triple TME modulation in a cold tumour (PMID 38438735).
- HIF-2α inhibition is now clinically validated in RCC (belzutifan vs everolimus,
  PMID 39167807) — the only pharmacological hypoxia-pathway success in the corpus.
- Glioblastoma: hypoxic macrophages are nominated as the therapeutic handle for
  vascular normalisation (PMID 38640932).

## What failed and why

CheckMate 143 (nivolumab vs bevacizumab in recurrent GBM) is the negative anchor
(PMID 32437507): neither immune de-repression nor anti-angiogenesis alone changes
survival in a tumour whose barrier is myeloid and anatomical. Anti-angiogenic dosing is
also dose-dependent in the wrong direction — excessive pruning worsens hypoxia, so
"normalisation windows" rather than maximal VEGF blockade is the operative concept
(PMID 29508855, PMID 36797231).

## Open question

There is no clinical assay for the normalisation window; dose and schedule are chosen
empirically, which plausibly explains inconsistent immunological benefit across tumour
types.
